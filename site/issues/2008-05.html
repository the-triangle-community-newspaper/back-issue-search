Community Garden in Cobargo
A group of neighbours and I
are trying to join ideas and efforts to
organise an Organic Community
Garden in Cobargo, for our town
and adjacent areas residents.
Despite having your own
veggie patch, we think it would be
important to be able to enjoy a
community project that could benefit
the whole population, from the very
young to the elderly.
The aim of the project is to ‘be
prepared’ for the future, when we
probably will not have access to the
means of transport we can use
today; also, the health of the
environment we are going to
transfer to our children will depend
on our care of the land, the water
and the natural/organic methods
used for cultivation.
The availability of the organic
garden will be possible if we can
form a committed group of people
from all ages, trades and expertise.
We need the young and strong
to do the physical work – with the
help perhaps of school children and
any able-bodied person with time on
their hands. Also crucial will be
those who can organise fund-raising,
administration, including
accountants and grant seekers. Once
we develop enough to be able to sell
our surplus produce to the public,
market attendants will also be in
demand as well as horticulturalists
for advice on biodiversity and
sustainability.
To participate in such
adventure we would like to include
children of any age and retired
people who will be able to work
with raised soil beds and attracting
native birds with the proper flora.
We have these ideas and more,
and we would like to welcome all
propositions in a meeting on Friday
the 23rd of May, at the CWA in
Cobargo, at 7.00 pm. Please, be there
if you care.
Rudolph Steiner said: “The
earth cannot withhold from men in
any age the fruitfulness that she can
give them, provided only that men
shall find those social forms which
will make it possible for what the
earth can give to flow in the right
way to humanity.”

What is it
with our main-
stream media?  It
was so refreshing
to get my copy of
The Triangle this
month, full of
good news stories, as well as the more
serious subjects.  The overall impres-
sion is that this is a vibrant, artistic
and caring community. Yet if you pick
up any of the large city newspapers,
particularly the tabloids, they all seem
to be confrontingly negative, especially
when it comes to the Rudd Govern-
ment.  So much so that Shaun Carney,
Senior Editor with the Melbourne Age
devoted half a page of last Saturday’s
edition questioning these journalists’
motives while stating the obvious that
this style of reporting did nothing for
the nation as a whole.
For instance, the now infamous
salute by Rudd to George Bush was on
the front page of every newspaper as
though he had been caught shaking the
hand of Bin Laden. Meanwhile, Brendan
Nelson is in the wings patronisingly
pontificating about bad manners while
the rest of what’s left of his government
are lecturing their opposites on how
they should do their jobs. All this nega-
tivity getting front page coverage in
every mainstream newspaper. As
Shaun Carney pointed out, they should
remember they were not born to rule
and it was his opinion that the front
bench of the new government were one
of the most intelligent this country has
seen.
But it doesn’t stop there. Take the
plight of our Indigenous people. It is ei-
ther grubby kids sniffing petrol or
drunks under a tree that we are pre-
sented with, but if we take the trouble
to watch some of the documentaries on
the ABC or SBS we would be confronted
with something that would make us
understand a little better the reason
that, against all odds, this remarkable
culture has survived.  Message Stick,
which is presented on the ABC at 6pm
on Fridays, at the moment is doing a
series of interviews with a group of the
most inspirational people you could
wish to meet. The interviewer, one
Miriam Corowa, is enchanting, intelli-
gent with that wonderful knack of
drawing the best out of her interview-
ees. Compulsive viewing in my opin-
ion.
But how much of this positive
approach do we get? It would seem that
the modern day journalist feels under
pressure from their bosses that unless
they flood our papers with sensational
garbage they won’t have a job. Of
course this is not so of all contributors.
We have some very skilled and inter-
esting writers in the mix but they are
certainly not in the majority.

Researchers from the University
of Wollongong have been out and about
in the Batemans Marine Park over the
last two months assessing the diver-
sity and abun-
dance of fish. This
is the second con-

marine research

Dr Kelaher
said the research is
one of a number of key components in
the Marine Park’s monitoring program
and will be ongoing. The researchers
use baited remote underwater video to
monitor fish life on reefs at a water
depth of between 30 to 40 metres.
One of the scientists leading the
research, Professor Andy Davis, said
that baited remote underwater video
is a particularly powerful technique
that allows reliable data to be collected
at the limits of safe scuba diving. This
year the researchers completed 96
drops for 30 minutes each time. This
means watching more than 48 hours
of television back in the laboratory to
accurately count the number of fish in
each site. Large numbers of drops are
required to ensure that the data is use-
ful.

New research has led to the dis-
covery of the heaviest element yet known
to science. The new element,
Governmentium (Gv), has one neutron, 25
assistant neutrons, 88 deputy neutrons,
and 198 assistant deputy neutrons, giv-
ing it an atomic mass of 312.
These 312 particles are held to-
gether by forces called morons, which
are surrounded by vast quantities of
lepton-like particles called peons. Since
Governmentium has no electrons, it is
inert; however, it can be detected, be-
cause it impedes every reaction with
which it comes into contact. A minute
amount of Governmentium can cause a
reaction that would normally take less
than a second to take from four days to
four years to complete.
Governmentium has a normal half-

An elderly lady actually wrote

Dear Sir,
I am writing to thank you for bounc-
ing my cheque with which I endeavoured to
pay my plumber last month. By my calcula-
tions, three ‘nanoseconds’ must have elapsed
between his presenting the cheque and the
arrival in my account of the funds needed to
honour it. I refer, of course, to the automatic
monthly deposit of my Pension, an arrange-
ment which, I admit, has been in place for
only eight years. You are to be commended
for seizing that brief window of opportu-
nity, and also for debiting my account £30
by way of penalty for the inconvenience
caused to your bank.
My thankfulness springs from the
manner in which this incident has caused
me to rethink my errant financial ways.
I noticed that whereas I personally
attend to your telephone calls and letters,
when I try to contact you, I am confronted
by the impersonal, overcharging, pre-re-
corded, faceless entity which your bank has
become. From now on I, like you, choose only
to deal with a flesh-and-blood person. My
mortgage and loan payments will therefore
and hereafter no longer be automatic, but
will arrive at your bank by cheque, addressed
personally and confidentially to an employee
at your bank whom you must nominate.
Be aware that it is an offence under
the Postal Act for any other person to open
such an envelope.
Please find attached an Application
Contact Status, which I require your chosen
employee to complete. I am sorry it runs to
eight pages, but in order that I know as much
about him or her as your bank knows about
me, there is no alternative. Please note that
all copies of his or her medical history must
be countersigned by a Solicitor, and the man-
datory details of his/her financial situation
(income, debts, assets and liabilities) must be
accompanied by documented proof.
In due course, I will issue your em-
ployee with a PIN number which he/she must
quote in dealings with me. I regret that it
cannot be shorter than 28 digits but, again, I
have modelled it on the number of button
presses required of me to access my account
balance on your phone bank service. As they
say, imitation is the sincerest form of flat-
tery.

further. When you call me, press buttons as

As pets enter their golden years, they can develop a
variety of conditions and diseases including weight and
mobility problems, arthritis, organ degeneration, cancers,
hormonal disorders, etc. Often we hear: “Oh, he’s just an
old dog”, implying that not much can help an
aging pet. Quite the opposite is true: with
proper care, many senior pets can be
rejuvenated, enjoying more energy and
comfort.
So when does a pet become Senior?
Generally, smaller dog breeds live longer
than larger breeds, and cats live longer
than dogs. Smaller dog breeds become
senior at 10-13 years; giant breeds become
seniors as young as five. Every dog or cat
year equals 5–7 human years.
A regular veterinary health check twice a
year is by far, the most important way to keep your
senior pet in top shape. During the check-up we note any
changes in your pet’s behaviour and condition, from top
to tail.
Nutrition & Dental Health   Senior pets have different
dietary needs, regarding weight control and nutrient
consumption.  Your pet may benefit from important
supplements such as flax seed oil and joint support
formulas.
Loss of appetite and foul breath may indicate dental
disease requiring veterinary attention.

Checking the Poop   Microscopic examination of your
pet’s faeces can identify problems with digestion,
internal bleeding, and pancreatic disorders. Most
importantly, these valuable tests can identify parasites:
roundworm, hookworm, whipworm, tapeworm
and giardia.
Wee troubles   A very common frustrating
but treatable problem is urinary
incontinence. Excessive urination or
incontinence may indicate diabetes or

if caught early enough.
Exercise for Senior Pets   Unexercised
bodies deteriorate more quickly. We can
prescribe an appropriate exercise program
to keep your pet fit—mentally and
physically—fun exercise will give you a boost too!
Senility in Pets   With the senior years comes a general
“slowing down” and dulling of senses. It’s important to
keep your pet active and stimulated. Aging pets may
deteriorate mentally, becoming confused, stressed and
showing signs of senility. These problems are treatable
with conventional or homoeopathic medicines.
It’s absolutely vital that we work together monitoring
physical and behavioural changes and supporting your pets’
changing needs to ensure they enjoy their Golden Years.

Garden Magic
Previously I’ve touched on soil
wetting agents and I feel as strongly on
this as the followers of Clean Energy
for Eternity do about their topic. 
Soil additives such as Wettasoil,
Saturaid and Rainsaver crystals are all
petroleum based. With petrol around
$1.50 a litre, do we
really need to be
exacerbating the
problem by wast-
ing this resource
in our gardens. Not to mention the long
term effects for worms. I don’t imagine
they can survive too long being doused
in these products. 
It is just as effective and much
more environmentally responsible to
simply improve the ground’s water
holding capacity by the addition of
compost and cow manure. Throw in
some gypsum if you have a heavy or
clay soil. Of course, you’ll get the maxi-
mum advantage if you mulch as well.
Sugarcane, leaf litter, bark, pebbles,
and so on all act as insulation to keep
the moisture in the earth. 
While we’re at it, herbicides
such as Roundup, Hortico weed killer
and so on are all the identical chemical
– glyphosate – usually at the same ra-
tio, ie. 360 g/litre. Of these, Eraze is by
far the best because it contains a dif-
ferent surfactant and therefore doesn’t
harm aquatic animals (frogs and fish).
So if you live on acres and risk run off
to your dam when spraying, use Eraze.

The Bermagui CTC is going from
strength to strength. Since we wrote
our last article for the Triangle we have
been able to see just how much differ-
ence moving into the newly refur-
bished Community Hall has made. The
Library – Information Centre – CTC
meld has been a welcome change and
has created a great communications
and information hub for visitors and
locals alike. Now for some advice on a
really common problem today:
Things you can do to prevent
spam
Never, ever reply to a spam mes-
sage. This includes buying a product
that is for sale or clicking the often-
misunderstood ‘unsubscribe’ link,
which actually informs your spammer
that you exist. If you can tell from the
subject line that a message is spam,
don’t open it — delete it. Spam subject
lines usually promise you a better sex
life, a more youthful appearance, pre-
scription drugs without a doctor’s ap-
proval, love, thicker hair, or a better
mortgage rate. They also use attention-
demanding punctuation, such as excla-
mation marks or all caps.
Don’t click any links in a spam
email. Spammers often have multiple,
unique pages on their sites. Often,
when you click a URL in a spam mes-
sage, this tells the spammer that you
— and only you — received the mes-
sage he or she sent.
Disguise your email address.
Don’t put your email address in plain
text on your Web site. An effective way
to trick the spiders that traverse the
Web to harvest email addresses is to
disguise your email address by strip-
ping out full stops and ‘@’ symbols. For
example, ‘YOURNAME AT YAHOO
DOT COM.’ You can also make the ‘@’
an image, which will prevent crawl-
ers from identifying it. You may also
wish to disguise it in your signature
file, in case your recipients forward
your email.
Don’t forward an email from
someone you don’t know to a list of
people. You remember those ‘forward
this email to 20 of your friends’ mes-
sages? They are perfect for spammers
to harvest email addresses, even if the
sender of the original email did not
have this intent. These types of sign-
and-forward emails often appear in the
form of a petition – and they don’t
work.
Don’t use your home or busi-
ness email address when you register
on a Web site or in a group. If you must
sign up for services, want to receive
more info, register for newspapers or
domains: use a free email address from
a site like Yahoo or Hotmail to create
an address especially for that purpose.
This also goes for posting to the Web,
in a listserve, newsgroup, on a contact
page for a Web site, or on a resume that
is posted on the Web.
Before you join a list, make sure
the list owner or Web master will not
sell your address. Check to see if you
can opt out of receiving unsolicited
email from the site where you’re regis-
tering. If you are unsure about this,
read the site’s privacy statement.
Preview your messages before
you open them. Outlook (and many
other email clients) let you use a pre-
view mode to peek at the contents of a
message before you actually open it.
To do this in Outlook, go the View menu
and select Preview Pane. Instead of
double clicking a message, click it once
to select it and you’ll see the message
displayed in the Preview Pane.
Use a complicated email
username. Spammers’ software will
look for the easy and obvious addresses
first, such as those with identifiable
names ‘john1977@hotmail.com,’ as op-
posed to ‘sjk839@msn.com
Never use your email address as
your screen name in chat rooms. It will
give spiders or human email harvest-
ers an absolute yes to a questionable
email address.

thing to do with technology.
