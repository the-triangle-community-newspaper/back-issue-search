A quiet change is occurring along
our coast as many small local groups
gather to take part in the South Coast
Estuary Health and Climate Change
Project. Overall this project is designed
to engage community and industry
groups and in our area to participate in
valuable research about the health of our
estuaries. It is being delivered and
coordinated by Suzzanne Gray and Allan
Broadhurst through Sulisit Consulting
(derived from Celtic Goddess “Sulis” -
Protector of Oceans, Rivers and
Streams), together with the Bermagui
Fishermen’s Co-operative and numerous
community groups.
It is delivering skills to local
interested people, who are prepared to
help with monitoring, protection and
assessment of our precious estuarine
ecosystems. After the February flooding
rain, many of our lakes including Wallaga
Lake and Cuttagee Lake opened to the
ocean and this year they are remaining
open. The cleansing impact of the oceanic
water and the welcome rains have
resulted in good fishing at Baragoot and
Wallaga Lake - evidenced by the
increased parking at the bridge and some
very healthy looking pelicans (this was
not part of the official program data
collection).
Healthy fish are due to more than
simply the entrance opening and a
flushing by marine waters.  It’s a
combination of things - the protection and
habitat offered by the estuaries, the
nutrients provided by catchment inputs
