Members of Bermagui Seasiders
Inc and their helpers have recovered

In the News
During the middle week of January,
just as many were emerging from the fog
of summer holidays, Cobargo was abuzz
with the news that their GP was retiring.
As the news filtered out, the phones ran
hot with everyone wondering if the GP
would be replaced and what would happen
if Cobargo could not attract another
doctor.
The need for a GP, especially with
many older people and young families in
the town and outlying areas, was
obviously the primary concern. The
single-doctor practice in Bermagui
certainly could not fill the void.
The second real concern was the
effect on the town. Would the pharmacy
survive without a doctor? Currently
Medicare will not issue a licence for a
pharmacy in a town of less than 2000
people. If we lose the chemist we will not
get another.
The flow-on would continue. If it
were necessary to travel for a GP and
chemist to Narooma or Bega, then
inevitably there would be a loss of trade
Sue Bear
The fragility of a country town
felt in other Cobargo businesses.
Naturally, the Cobargo Co-operative,
which owns the building, is committed
to its continued use as a doctor’s
surgery.
Early in the week the Southern
General Practice Network (SGPN) was
contacted to ascertain the assistance
this organization could give.
The SGPN is a Federally-funded
network of general practice whose role
is to support GPs and their practice staff
by assisting with recruitment, training
and practice management.
On Friday 21st January
the SGPN convened a meeting
in Cobargo with the CEO of the
Division and Dr Lee to discuss
the continuity of the Practice.  By
Monday 23rd the Division’s
Workforce Officer, Ms Sue Berry
reported to The Triangle that
negotiations were under way
and a couple of options were
being considered. Ms Berry said,
“We are working with Dr Lee
and are confident that a solution
will be found”, and Cobargo will continue
to have the services of a GP.
This issue, although close to a
solution, has brought home the financial
fragility of small towns. Support for local
businesses makes sense in a world of
ever-increasing fuel prices, and flood or
drought affected food prices.
Cobargo has always been a
“working town,” achieving much with its
strong sense of community.
Let’s keep improving services
locally and keep our town developing –
slowly - just the way we like it.

Editorial
The main summer burst has been and gone by the looks of things, ie a few
hot days in between all the cloud and rain.  Am sure we all hope for an Indian
Summer to make up for what we have missed, but we still have to remind ourselves
that this time last year, we were all suffering from excessive heat and drought and
worried about fires, so enjoy this wonderful green scenario whilst it lasts.
Our far south corner seems to have been blessed with a lot of visitors over
the main summer holidays.  Whether this has resulted in good trading for the
businesses and accommodation remains to be seen and a small independent
survey has been undertaken by one of our trusty reporters which is elsewhere in
our paper.  Sapphire Coast Tourism also has a survey card which can be handed
out to visitors and is available at your local Visitor Centre.
Many of our visitors have taken part in the Bare Foot Bowls at the Country
Club and businesses are being invited to enter into a follow up to this, a competition
with good prizes so that you can support your local Country Club whilst having fun.
Over 100 visitors took part some weeks during the Christmas holidays in the
evening bowls and it was lovely to see kids of all ages having such a great time.
Our front page features the results of the much wanted and lobbied for car
park in Cobargo. It was a badly executed job. There are rumours afloat that
Dickinson Park may be destined for similar treatment. Having survived an
excessively wet summer, there can be no justification for more tar and cement in
Bermagui, and the destruction of a lovely and unique foreshore. Imagine the heat!
The Art Trail has been alive and well this summer with some excellent
exhibitions in Bermagui and on the road to Tathra.  It is great to see the Bermagui
Community Centre being used as a regular venue for small exhibitions and it is
hoped this space will be expanded with more tracking & lighting in the main hall this
year.
The commercial galleries, Ivy Hill & Narek have been supported by a small
photography exhibition by 10 local photographers at Indaba, on the Four Winds
road and the new gallery Shop7ArtSpace next to the Horse and Camel has had
some interesting mixed media exhibitions as has The Bermagui Country Club.
February is Cobargo Show month, a great little show on the 11th and 12th – get
your produce ready – followed by the open air cinema the next weekend at the
Cobargo Swimming Pool- the Empire Strikes Back – and then the finale, the Cobargo
Folk Festival on the last weekend. Programmes for both the Cobargo Show and the
Cobargo Folk Festival are around the Triangle.
See you there.

At some stage in your gardening
life you will want to grow plants in
containers. My best advice is to buy the
best potting mix you can afford.
Most potting mixes are based on
composted pinebark and the cheap and
nasty generic ones are just that
...nasty. Often you will find large chunks
of pinebark that haven’t broken down yet,
and this is a disaster for your plants.
Nitrogen is needed to continue the
breakdown process and it will be
“robbed” from the mix leaving your
plants to starve, the result being pale
unhealthy plants with yellowing leaves.
The best potting mixes are based on peat
or coir peat (coconut fibre ), as these
mediums hold onto nutrients and
moisture while still allowing good
drainage.
NEVER use potting mixes as soil
improvers. I get a lot of customers
wanting to improve their soils or give their
newly planted seedlings a head start by
planting them in a handful of potting mix.
Potting mix is
designed to work in a
container and has all the
opposite properties of
what is required in the
soil. It is much better, and
usually 
more 
cost
effective 
to 
use
mushroom compost, cow
manure , soil conditioner
or a compost mix in the
garden.
If you are potting
into a very large pot or
container you can add up
to 20% good garden soil
to your good quality
potting mix to keep costs
down a bit, but avoid soil in smaller pots
as 
it 
can 
create 
havoc 
with
drainage when it continually dries out
and is rewet, You can also add well
composted animal manures to potting
mixes but just be aware the more organic
matter in the mix the more it will “slump”
and drop in the pot as it breaks down
and will require regular repotting or
topping up.
Finally, be aware of drainage
when gardening in pots. Always “crock”
the pot, this means adding aggregate in
the form of broken pottery, gravel
crushed tiles etc to the bottom of the pot
before adding potting mix, to avoid
drainage holes being blocked.
Happy potting!!

Bread and Butter Pickles
2 kg cucumbers peeled or unpeeled
and sliced finely
3 medium onions sliced
1 green pepper sliced (optional)
2 cloves garlic, whole, in muslin bag
(optional)
Put the sliced vegetables in a large
bowl and pour over a brine made
from about  2 tablespoons salt in 2
cups water. Let stand for 3 hours then
drain and rinse.
Boil together 2 and a half cups cider
vinegar with the same quantity of
sugar, 1 tablespoon mustard seeds, 1
teaspoon tumeric and the garlic if
desired. Remove garlic. Mix liquid
with the drained vegetables and
bottle. I use Fowler’s jars and process
in a hot water bath at 92 deg.C
ensuring a safe vacuum seal.
Beetroot can be processed in the
same way. I partially cook whole
beetroot so that the skin slips off
easily, then slice them into jars. The
pickling liquid to cover 5 or 6 large
beetroot is a tangy mixture of 1 and a
half cups white vinegar, 3 quarters of
a cup of water and the same of sugar,
half a teaspoon each of peppercorns,
cloves, mustard seeds and salt , a bay
leaf and half a cinnamon stick.
