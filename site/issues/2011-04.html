events, which I am sure the Triangle and

be studying somewhere in France, and will
find out who his host family is only in the

about how strangers meet, but in

to Vanessa Milton’s decision to include the
story in “Portrait of a Stranger”.

It’s 9.30am on Monday morning, at the beginning of the
new term, and I’m standing at the door to the Year 3 classroom
at Cobargo Public School.
“Could Alice* please come for reading?” I ask Mrs Martin.
Mrs Martin glances over at Alice, who’s sitting in the back
row, looking worried. There follows a few minutes of whispered
negotiations between teacher and student, before Alice agrees
to go with me to the room we use for MULTILIT.
MULTILIT (Making Up Lost Time In LITeracy) is a
programme devised by Macquarie University for students who
need to catch up to their age reading level. Research has shown
that most students taught by MULTILIT methods make
extraordinary gains in literacy skills in a short period of time.
Students in our programme spend 30 minutes working
one-on-one with a YESS reading tutor 3-4 times a week.
Volunteers in the local YESS initiative (Youth Educational Support
Service) have been tutoring students at Narooma High and
Cobargo and Bermagui Primary schools for the past eight
months or so. I, for one, find it very satisfying to see the kids
improve, and to see their relief and their sense of achievement
as the letters on the page start to make sense. Yes, they can
read!
YESS volunteers spend up to 1½ hours one day a week
with children at Narooma High, Cobargo Public or Bermagui
Public schools. Some are retired, some are parents or
grandparents, some are just community members who like to
help kids with reading.
If you’d like to be involved or wish to find out more, visit
www.yess.org.au or call Frances Perkins  on  6493 6486. We’d
love to talk to you, as more volunteers would mean we can bring
more kids into the programme.
It’s two weeks later and I’m standing at the door of Year
3 again. Alice spots me and she’s up and out the door before I
even knock, chatting about her weekend as we make our way to
the reading room. Her reading is improving already and she
knows it, and what’s more she’s enjoying it. And that’s what brings
me back, week after week.

WHAT IS A COMMUNITY WORTH TO YOU
Sometimes we like the anonymity of the big smoke and not having to say
‘Hello’ 15 times when you are in a hurry to get the mail and disappear home but that
said, there have been many remarks lately about what a wonderful community we
have in this area.
One of the best things in our Communities is  the smiling  faces acknowledging
one’s presence in town and genuine enquiries as to one’s well being.
You can disappear behind the paper if you do not wish to chat whilst having
a coffee, on the other hand, you could be feeling lonely that day and need that chat.
There will always be someone to indulge your needs and there will always be a
great cup of coffee.
Have you been sick and not into the cooking? Someone will arrive on your
doorstep with a delicious home made offering for you and /or your family to tide you
over.  Do you need driving to the village? That is not a problem either.
Have you realized that so many good events happen in our Triangle, including
our regular large Festivals in Bermagui, Cobargo and Tilba. These festivals have
their own International stars not forgetting the generosity of Genevieve Lacey and
Poul Hoxbro  the virtuoso recordist, performing free of charge on their only night off
during a world tour.  How lucky can we be!!   We have not mentioned the regular art
shows, choirs, plays and impromptu gigs, so give thanks for our Triangle and may it
continue to have its atmosphere permeating through our small villages for years to
come.
