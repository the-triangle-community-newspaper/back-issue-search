Men: Do you know if you have a Spanner in the Works?
During International Men’s Health 
Week in June, Wallaga Lake Bermagui 
Men’s Shed is organising a series of free 
health checks as part of a national program 
called ‘Spanner in the Works?’
‘Spanner in the Works?’ plays on the 
idea that men often pay more attention to 
the health and well-being of their vehicles 
than their own health and well-being. 
Many men remain reluctant to talk about 
their health and too often delay or avoid 
going to see a GP to get any potential 
problems checked out—an attitude that 
heavily contributes to the fact that men 
die on average five years earlier than 
women. Across Australia, many men have 
been identified with serious conditions 
and advised to seek immediate treatment 
through participating in this program.
This month, Wallaga Lake Bermagui 
Men’s Shed, in cooperation with the 
clinical staff of Bermagui Medical Centre, 
will conduct a ‘Spanner in the Works?’ 
program. This will take place in the 
Bermagui Country Club where men of 
all ages are welcome to drop in, NO 
APPOINTMENT NECESSARY, to meet 
confidentially with a clinical specialist. The 
dates, times and location are below.
In addition, and to promote 
International men’s Health Week and 
increase the awareness of men’s mealth 
issues, Dr Muller-Grotjan of the Bermagui 
Medical Centre will present a talk, ‘Healthy 
Body – Healthy Mind: Keeping the 
Balance’ on Tuesday 6 June from 5 pm – 7 
pm at Bermagui Country Club. This is open 
to ALL Members of the public, men and 
all women who have men in their life that 
they care for. This presentation will provide 
an overview of men’s health issues and is 
coordinated by the Wallaga Lake Bermagui 
Men’s Shed and U3A Bermagui & District.
By reaching out to men locally in the 
community and fostering an atmosphere 
in which they feel comfortable, the project 
hopes to influence good habits in men and 
help bridge the gap between men’s and 
women’s health. Of course, this check-up 
should not be seen as a replacement for a 
complete check-up by your GP and is more 
about encouraging men to start thinking 
more preventatively and acting more 
proactively about their health. 
‘Spanner in the Works?’ is fully 
supported by the Federal Department of 
Health and Ageing. Please put the dates 
in your diary:
“Spanner in the Works?” - Free Health 
Check and Advice for all Men.

View on Huntfest
Dear Editors,
Conversations with visitors  to 
Narooma over the Easter and Anzac Day 
holidays convinced me that they knew 
little or nothing about HuntFest. They were 
amazed that such an unsuitable event as a 
celebration of hunting and killing animals 
should have been given a licence by the 
Eurobodalla Shire Council to operate on 
crown land in the main street of Narooma 
every June long weekend to 2022. 
HuntFest has been encouraged by 
local council without community support or 
debate, to satisfy the wishes of the local 
Hunters Club, backed by the Sporting 
Shooters Association of Australia from 
outside the Shire. It has grown over three 
years from a photographic competition 
to a full scale American-style festival of 
hunting with sale of guns, promotion of 
overseas and local safaris, a shooting range, 
and free admission and special activities 
for children aimed to convince them that 
gun ownership is desirable and killing 
animals is fun. 
A festival of killing birds and animals, 
however much it may be disguised as just 
another family-friendly activity, or given 
the incongruous  title of “conservation 
hunting”, can only be harmful  to the 
wellbeing of the community, to native 
animals, and to our well-earned reputation 
as the “Nature Coast”.
Citizens opposed to gun proliferation 
and wishing to add their voices to those 
of South Coast residents and organisations 
like the Australian Medical Association, the 
RSPCA, the Alannah and Madeline 
Foundation and Coastwatchers might do 
so by appealing widely to government 
departments and ministers such as  the 
NSW Minister for Crown Lands, Gabrielle 
Upton, and the NSW Premier, Gladys 
Berejiklian.
State and federal politicians  still 
have the responsibility  to use the 
overarching  influence of their 
governments  in order to have 
the undemocratic and dangerous 
“ H u n t F e s t ” l i c e n c e r e s c i n d e d . 
Unfortunately they are reluctant to 
intervene with decisions made by local 
councils unless there is an ugly incident, 
demonstrably flawed decision making, or a 
strong demonstration of public opposition. 
Speak out before it’s too late and a 
child is injured by a gun sold from a public 
building in the heart of Narooma, before 
native animals are shot by recreational 
hunters in the surrounding forests, and 
holiday makers, tourists and home-makers 
decide to stay away from a place that that 
they can no longer regard as an idyllic 
destination. “How Life Should Be”, not 
“Hunters’ Headquarters”. 
Susan Cruttenden

HIGH COUNTRY
I wake in the night
straining to hear the first bird song,
skin prickling in the high fragile air
in the pregnant seconds before the 
dawn ...
A wild dog howls ...
his voice of power, lifting
to the tissue thin sky
‘I am lord ~
 the law of the land abides’.
And I shiver as the call of the wild
stands up the hair on the back of my 
neck.
Softly, pale slanting light drifts 
through
snow gum, black sally and wattle
and strides in great lines
across the vast windblown plain
to the barrier of stark, motionless hills.
The sun shifts on through space and 
time
lifting the face of golden buttercups,
and daisies in everlasting shades, 
violets,
massed peabush with egg-and-bacon 
hues
a chaos of colour to paint the day.
And at night, 
At night a heaven glitters like a swarm 
of fireflies
splashing a milky way frothing in its 
own intensity
across an arc as wide as a double span 
of arms,
~ a brumby, eerie in the darkness
trots off to a ghostly range - and 
silence
and the land renews itself for another 
tomorrow.
Judi Hearn

Tilba RFS Report
We have now entered that time of 
year when precautionary measures are 
implemented for next season, both by 
property owners and various authorities. 
This includes tidying up around your home 
and property, and pile burns, and the large 
scale hazard reduction burns by RFS, and 
others including NPWS and Forestry. 
Property owners are reminded that whilst 
a permit is generally not required during 
the winter period, it remains a requirement 
that you advise your local Fire Control 
Centre (Moruya 4474 2855 or Bega 6494 
7400, depending upon which shire you are 
in) twenty four hours beforehand, as well 
as all neighbours. Anyone in Tilba area 
who would like us to assist on a pile burn, 
please ring.
Tilba Brigade has assisted with two 
Hazard Reduction (HR) burns so far this 
season, both in the Narooma area. We 
recognise that smoke can be annoying and 
disappointing to many, but HR burns are a 
good practice in that they help protect our 
community. They have to be done when 
conditions are favourable.
Tilba RFS held its AGM on 9 May. 
At that meeting Tralee Snape stepped 
down from the position of Senior Deputy 
Captain to Deputy Captain, and was 
replaced by Dave Alabaster. The meeting 
was especially significant for the fact that 
Richard Bate was honoured by Captain, 
Bruce Allen, who presented him with 
a Certificate of Appreciation on the 
occasion of his stepping down from the 
Field Officer position. His career has 
included 32 continuous years as a Field 
Officer, including 24 years as Deputy 
Captain, three years as Senior Deputy 
Captain, and five years as Captain. The 
Brigade members were pleased to be able 
to witness and support this recognition of 
his long years of service and were pleased 
to see appreciation expressed in this way. 
Richard will continue as an active member 
of the Brigade.
This is a powerful illustration of the 
fact that our members are ageing. Three 
of our active members are over 70 years 
of age. We need new members to come 
forward or Tilba Fire Brigade will be in 
danger of being unable to function. We are 
a volunteer organisation, and I ask once 
again for new people to come and see us 
and explore the opportunity to join and 
support the community. I can be phoned 
at any time on 4473 7259. If no answer, 
please leave a message.
Harry Bate

Reclaim the Riverbank – Did you know?
On my walk this morning I 
encountered Toppy Blacka and together 
we walked from the Quaama cemetery 
down the track along the riverbank and 
back up to Rob’s corner.
What an interesting walk it was. 
Toppy told me a few things I didn’t 
know. She has lived in this area all 
her life and has seen many changes. 
Many years ago the river flat was used 
for community sporting events. There 
was an Easter meet, where the locals 
had foot races and all manner of other 
sporting events. The river flat was kept 
mown, just like a sports ground, right 
up until the new sports ground was built 
on the other side of the highway. 
Once the sporting events 
relocated, the river flat reverted to 
native vegetation, until two of Toppy’s 
grandsons wanted a bike track. Armed 
with hand tools, they cleared a path all 
the way through from Rob’s corner up 
to the cemetery. And that is the path 
we still walk today and are working to 
reclaim from honeysuckle and other 
weeds.
 If you took the walk today, you 
would notice great improvements. At 
our last working bee Graham came 
down with his slasher, and Chris with 
his brush cutter, and between them they 
mowed around previous plantings and 
cleared a couple of areas so we can 
establish more canopy plants. Peter 
tackled the honeysuckle, and Sangito 
and Rose did micro weeding.
Barney did some amazing 
earthworks to level a wombat mound 
to make it safe to walk across, and he 
also placed a warning barrier, so there 
is no doubt of the hazard created by 
wombat mansions.
It was a great effort by a small 
team with good results. Toppy Blacka, 
who is 81 years old, managed the walk 
without any trouble. She was very 
pleased to be able to access the path, and 
it brought back some good memories 
for her.
Next working bee: 4 June 9.30–
11.30 am. Bring gloves, hat, weeding 
and pruning tools. See you there.
Rose Chaffey

Another chance to say “No” to Woolies’ BWS
On 4 May 2017 neighbours of 
Bermagui Woolworths received a notice 
that the application to transfer the packaged 
liquor licence from Pam’s Store, Tilba 
Tilba, to the shop beside the supermarket 
had been lodged with the NSW Independent 
Liquor and Gaming Authority (ILGA). 
This shop is to trade as “BWS - Beer 
Wine Spirits”. This application, including 
a Community Impact Statement, has not 
as yet appeared on the ILGA Noticeboard.
As noted previously on the front 
page of the February 2017 issue of The 
Triangle, “Woolworths won’t take no for 
an answer on liquor licence”, Woolworths 
had a previous application for a new liquor 
licence at this location formally refused as 
recently as January 2016. 
In the previous application process in 
2015, 55 of 57 local resident submissions 
opposed the application. This level of 
local opposition carried some weight 
with the ILGA, so we encourage you to 
make a submission again this time. The 
application is expected to be posted on the 
ILGA Noticeboard shortly (Google “ILGA 
noticeboard” and enter “Bermagui”), 
and generally there is 30 days to make 
submissions.
Nothing has changed in Bermagui 
of any note that should provide any 
justification to approve this transfer

Horizon celebrating 20 years in Bermagui
May 2017 marked the 20th 
anniversary of the opening of Horizon 
Credit Union’s branch in the Bermagui 
community. 
As the credit union of choice in the 
Bega Valley region, Horizon has been 
supporting the Bermagui community 
since 1997 with a variety of finance and 
banking services.
In the late nineties, rural towns 
like Bermagui were being left without 
banking and financial services due to the 
closure of major banks in the area. 
With little notice of the closures, 
small business struggled as residents 
sought out other locations to bank and 
consequently shop.
 The heavy reliance on cash also 
limited people’s ability to save, leaving 
many families facing financial hardship.
“When we were left without a bank, 
I was very concerned with how it was 
going to impact the town,” said Janette 
Neilson, a Bega Valley Shire councillor 
at the time.
Bega Valley Shire Council 
recognised the dire situation Bermagui 
was in. Horizon Credit Union agreed to 
open a branch in Bermagui with Council 
agreeing to fund it during its first two 
years of operation.
Once news was out that Horizon 
Credit Union would be opening a branch 
in Bermagui, locals were eager to find out 
about Horizon’s financial products and 
services and to support it by closing their 
bank accounts and opening a membership 
at Horizon. 
Strong community support is still 
evident 20 years on, as Horizon currently 
services 86% of the Bermagui population.

Bermagui’s many benefactors
What makes some people care enough 
to want to make a difference, to give their 
time and/or resources to create a better 
community? Bermagui has always been 
fortunate to attract new residents who care. 
Sam Sinclair stands out in the crowd. 
He arrived on his pushbike in 1906 and 
became legendary for his generosity to 
people and the community. He was a boxer, 
footballer, bush dentist, barber and weigh 
master. He adopted a Down syndrome 
baby, built the Kings Hall and aged care 
units, made coffins … and his taxi doubled 
as the hearse. These are just a few of his 
achievements. 
Sam put everything he had back into 
the community he loved. Bill Dickinson 
came to Bermagui in the 1930s and after 
observing the longevity of the people 
became a permanent boarder at the Hotel. 
Bill financed the construction of the Blue 
Pool and the sports oval which is his 
namesake. When Sister Harris retired 
from nursing she spent her days and 
nights devoted to the health and welfare 
of a community, without a doctor. Ron 
Gaha and Judi Hearn were founding 
members of the Bermagui Historical 
Society and spent many years compiling 
and publishing historical facts. Judi is still 
actively recording our history at Montreal 
Goldfields and after 24 years’ commitment 
is surely our most informed historian. 
The Bermagui Museum preserves 
these stories and many more. But like all 
volunteer-run organisations we struggle 
to keep the doors open due to a lack of 
active members. If you are interested in 
the story of the Djiringanj people and 
European settlement of the Triangle area, 
like communicating with the general public 
and would like to be part of a great little 
modern museum, I’d love to hear from 
you; please contact me on 0419 173 607.

Norma’s years of service recognised
On Tuesday, 9 May 2017 the Cobargo 
branch of the CWA honoured its Patron, 
Norma Allen, with a well-deserved Life 
Membership of the CWA, at a lunch and 
presentation held in the CWA Cottage. 
Members of her family were also 
there to see her receive her certificate and 
badge from the Branch President, Lynne 
Jones.
Mrs Norma Allen has been a dedicated 
and loyal member of the Cobargo CWA for 
69 years; her first meeting was recorded on 
7 May 1948.
Norma is renowned throughout the 
state for her connection to Land Cookery, 
and she was in her early 20s when she 
started judging. Over six decades she has 
cut, prodded, snapped, sniffed and tasted 
her way through thousands of cakes, 
biscuits, slices and scones at shows, 
including the Sydney Royal for more than 
20 years. As a result, she was awarded a 
life membership of the Royal Agricultural 
Society of NSW.
She has appeared on radio, and on 
television with media personality Andrew 
Denton, where she said, “I would not give 
my mother a prize if the entry wasn’t 100 
per cent.”
 Norma also sat on the organising 
committee of The Land Cookery 
competition for many years and travelled 
the state, giving workshops and seminars.
She is still competing. Her first entry 
into show competition was a ‘Boiled 
Pudding’, and, this year again, her ‘Boiled 
Pudding’ has been selected to represent the 
area at the State level competition.
Around Cobargo, Norma has 
volunteered for many groups and cooked 
for families when they were ill or in need. 
On the benefit of volunteering Norma 
says: “It is not what you gather, but what 
you scatter that tells what kind of life you 
have lived.”

Vets’ special offers to de-sex your pet this month
Animal Welfare League NSW Far 
South Coast branch is hoping to stop the 
needless birth of unwanted kittens in the 
Bega Valley Shire. Until the end of June, 
people unable to afford the cost of de-
sexing their cats can do so for only $50. 
In the past the focus has been primarily 
on de-sexing of female cats, but “it 
takes two to tango”. So subsidies will 
be provided for both male and female 
cats. After all, male cats can impregnate 
many females each day!
While there has been huge support 
from the majority of the community in 
response to the plea to de-sex their pets, 
there are still those who, for various 
reasons, have still not de-sexed their 
pets. The branch is hoping to dispel 
some of the many myths surrounding 
de-sexing and to encourage all members 
of our community to de-sex not only 
their cats, but their dogs as well. 
Many people are unaware that cats 
can produce kittens as early as five months, 
that litter mates can breed and cats can 
be fertile immediately after giving birth. 
Others are unaware of the benefits of de-
sexing their cats:
•	
Un-desexed pets are driven 
hormonally to roam, potentially 
getting involved in car accidents, 
fights and undesirable behaviour 
such as chewing, digging and general 
frustration if contained whilst 
hormones are raging. 
•	
De-sexed pets will be less prone to 
territorial behaviour such as spraying 
indoors and “heat” cycles in females 
are eliminated. 
•	
Allowing your cat to use its 
reproductive organs greatly increases 
its risk of developing various cancers 
such as testicular cancer, prostate 
cancer/disorders in males, cystic 
ovaries, ovarian tumours, acute 
uterine infections, mammary cancer 
and tumours, as well as physical and 
nutritional exhaustion in females if 
continually breeding. 
•	
There is also the huge cost of 
rearing and then re-homing this litter 
as kittens must be vaccinated and 
microchipped before being given 
away or sold. Many go on to produce 
litters themselves as many are never 
de-sexed. Last year 84% of the cats 
and kittens surrendered to the branch 
were not de-sexed.
•	
Many unwanted kittens are given 
away free to go on to produce litters 
of their own or are at risk of becoming 
prey to those who would use them as 
prey in illegal “blood sports”. 
Dumping of kittens also has 
an ethical cost, as well as being 
illegal and inhumane. Non-owned “stray” 
animals, or feral cats as some refer 
to them, had an “owner” at one time!

advantage of the “Half Price De-sexing 
Offer” available at all Bega Valley Shire 
vets until the end of June.

A herb for all seasons
With the ever-increasing demand for 
food to be labelled with the origin of the 
supply, one sure method of knowing where 
it is grown is to grow your own.
A great way of doing this, with the 
bonus of trying new and exciting 
taste sensations, is to use the amazing 
number of herbs that can be grown 
easily in our own backyards.
Herbs are tolerant of a wide 
range of growing conditions and 
they grow well with very little 
maintenance. However, for the best 
results it is best to categorise them 
into four groups. If you follow the 
growing hints for each group, your 
herbs should be healthy, flavoursome 
and fragrant.
Initially though, the preparation 
of the site is most important with a 
light, well-drained soil and generally in 
full sun. Some herbs prefer a light shade.
Before planting, dig the soil to about 
25cm deep. If the soil lacks any decent 
humus it is a good idea to add some 
well-rotted cow or chicken manure and 
either some blood and bone or a complete 
fertiliser. You can also add some garden 
lime at the rate of half a cup per square 
metre. Fork all this together and probably 
let it rest for a week to ten days. 
Getting back to the four categories of 
herbs, they are: 
•	
Mediterranean Herbs
•	
High Summer Moisture Requirers
•	
Vegetable Culture Herbs, and 
•	
those that will Suit Most Conditions.
The Mediterranean group includes 
thymes, sages, lavender, marjoram, 
rosemary, french tarragon, lemon verbena 
and several others. These herbs will handle 
fairly low soil fertility and higher lime 
or alkaline soils. They require very good 
drainage hence lower water requirements.
The next category, High Summer 
Moisture Requirers, includes the mints, 
water cress, lemon balm, lemon Grass, 
sorrel, cardamom and also several others. 
These herbs prefer a soil with high nutrient 
and will not perform well if allowed to dry 
out. Saying that though, the soil must be 
well drained and not water-logged—apart 
from the mints that will perform in wet 
conditions.
Thirdly, the Vegetable Culture Herbs 
include basil, chives, parsley, rocket, 
coriander, dill, fennel and several others. 
These herbs again like high soil nutrition 
with the focus being on high nitrogen rates. 
They also prefer good summer moisture.
The last group are the Suit Most 
Conditions herbs. These include borage, 
feverfew, tansy, winter tarragon and aloe 
vera, to name a few. These will handle the 
conditions of all the above.
Therefore, when planning your herb 
garden it is important to divide your 
garden into the four categories mentioned. 
This will make it much easier to maintain 
and ensure that each herb gets the best 
conditions for healthy growth. A 
variety of foliage types within each 
group will also make the garden 
visibly more appealing.
•	 Other factors to consider in the 
location of the garden:
•	 Have it close to the kitchen so 
quick access is available.
•	 Use it regularly so fresh material 
is always coming on.
•	 Replace plants like parsley every 
two years as they often can go 
to seed.
Herbs come in perennial and 
annual forms with the perennial 
types usually dying down over winter. As 
these die back give them a cut back, and in 
early spring spread a handful of complete 
fertiliser around them with a few handfuls 
of composted cow manure. This will get 
them off to a good start.  Annual forms will 
require replacement every year. 
For those of you who have difficulty in 
providing a plot for your herbs, remember 
that they do very well in containers. Just 
follow the guidelines above and keep 
like requiring plants together in the same 
container. Mints can become invasive and 
are best grown in a pot under the backyard 
tap. 
Remember, always use a premium 
potting mix in the potting process and 
regularly feed with a seaweed/fish emulsion 
fertiliser.
Happy herbing

Choko Pickle
I read with interest Keith’s article about chokos in 
last month’s Triangle. However, I think he forgot the best 
use for them, ie pickles. Try this and judge for yourself.
1.5kgs chokos
1kg onions
1/2 cup salt
1 litre brown vinegar
1 cup plain flour
2 cups sugar
1 dessertspoon tumeric
1 dessertspoon mustard powder
1 dessertspoon curry powder
1/2 tsp ground ginger
Peel and slice chokos and onions and sprinkle with 
salt. Leave to stand overnight, then strain and rinse the next 
morning. Place in a saucepan with the vinegar and simmer 
until tender.Mix the dry ingredients to a paste with a small 
amount of vinegar, then add to the choko mixture, bringing 
it back to the boil for about 5 minutes. Cool a little, then 
bottle into clean glass jars.
Melting Apple Custard
Now that apples are at their best it’s 
a good time to use them for cooking as 
well as eating fresh.
1.5kg tart cooking apples, peeled and 
cored
100g unsalted butter
1 cup sugar
90ml Calvados (optional)
1 vanilla bean, split
3 large eggs
2 large egg yolks
300ml double cream
1. Preheat oven to 220C
2. Cut the apples in quarters. Heat 
the butter in a pan over medium high 
heat. When butter is hot and foamy add 
the apples and sauté them until golden, 
about 10 minutes. Add half the sugar and 
cook until it has caramelised, shaking the 
pan so that the sugar doesn’t burn. If using 
Calvados, pour into the pan and swirl it 
around then flame, gently shaking it until 
the flames die down.
3. Transfer the apples to a 1.5 litre 
casserole. 
4. Scrape the seeds from the vanilla 
bean. Whisk together the eggs and egg 
yolks in a medium bowl, then whisk in 
the vanilla seeds. Whisk in the remaining 
sugar and cream until combined. Pour the 
mixture over the apples and bake in the 
centre of the oven until the top is golden 
and puffed, about 35 minutes. 
Let cool for about 20 minutes before 
serving. 
Enjoy.
