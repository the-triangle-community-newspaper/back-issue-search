Possum plague closes Quaama Hall
Despite ongoing efforts by the Hall 
Committee, the number of possums 
residing in Quaama Hall seems to be 
growing. While it was kind of cute when 
we had one or two, there is now a village 
in there with at least five animals spotted 
and surely more likely to be hiding in the 
wings.
The internal ceiling that was recently 
replaced is now showing stains from 
what is dripping though. Their droppings 
aren’t just visible there! The whole place 
has puddles and piles as the animals are 
running rampant around the building each 
and every night.
The Hall Committee President, Hans 
Wiedeman, has closed the hall because of 
this unsanitary state of affairs. There will 
be no more dances, meetings, rehearsals or 
weddings until the situation is fixed.
Securing the building is now the 
committee’s top priority. They are worried 
that the blighters could chew through 
a wire and start a fire. The Council is 
standing with us and coughing up the 
funding to see that every hole is plugged. 
Once the last possum has packed its bags 
and left for good with no means of return, 
the whole building will be cleaned properly 
before re-opening. We’ll keep you posted. 
Bhagya

Narira Creek, Cobargo
We drive through drowned towns, cross 
Narira Creek 
into Cobargo just around midday 
when the sky is low and the light is weak.
There’s water thundering down the creek bed 
where I’ve never seen water flowing before. 
It froths and flings itself against the bank. 
‘Let’s stop,’ I say. ‘Is it safe here?’ ‘Oh, sure.’ 
We park our scarlet car beside the bridge 
and slosh through puddles out into the 
middle, 
gaze at the tangle of sodden foliage 
our umbrellas up against the drizzle. 
Matchstick timber tumbles in the torrent 
of chocolate-coloured mud so deep and 
dank, 
oozing downriver with hustling current. 
‘Once, when I was just a girl,’ I confide, 
‘I rode right down there, where the water 
flows. 
I was so young; it was so long ago.’ 
Two girls ride horses in shimmering air –
tall chestnut gelding and small grey pony. 
The thin girl is dark, the other one fair. 
Horses’ hooves strike burning bitumen 
as, lolling bareback, the girls roll through 
town, 
high soprano voices calling like birds 
the only other sounds as they go down. 
Lurching down creek bank with tight reins 
in hand 
to where thirsty willows trail their branchlets, 
picking their way across pool-pocked sand 
all moisture now drunk by the greedy sun. 
The girls drop their reins and they amble 
where they will on the way across the creek 
on a summer, Sunday morning ramble. 
Ginger’s startled by the ‘ark’ of a crow; 
and he is off towards a distant hill, 
thundering out of the dust he goes, 
Buttons and her rider chasing grimly. 
The girl on Ginger’s back clutches his mane, 
legs clinging to the sweat-flecked, heaving 
flanks 
bellowing for help from her friend in vain. 
At last the horse tires of his headlong flight 
with hapless jockey bouncing on his back, 
suddenly slows then stops, lets her alight 
sliding down his side with pounding heartbeat. 
Buttons and her rider catch up at last 
And the girls roll laughing on the grass: 
‘I didn’t know Ginger could run so fast!’ 
Kookaburra sitting on a fence post 
guffaws at the show they are putting on 
as if he is never going to stop. 
On the bridge, I look at my wrinkled hand 
holding umbrella against the slanting rain. 
‘I was so young; it was so long ago.’ 
Pauline O’Carolan

New environmental programs taking off at Quaama Public School
The students, staff and parents of 
Quaama Public School are implementing 
a number of new initiatives this year as a 
means of broadening their work within 
environmental education. 
A gardening club has been established 
to give students the opportunity to learn 
about and care for the wide assortment of 
plants, shrubs and trees that are within the 
school. Led by School Learning Support 
Officer Jan Whyte, this has become a 
popular lunchtime activity. 
Secondly, a recent grant from the 
Bowerbird Cobargo Community Op Shop 
has enabled the school to begin Stage 1 of 
their orchard project. The program, which 
is being co-ordinated by school parent 
Jasmine Ventura, will see a fully enclosed 
orchard constructed, containing a wide 
variety of fruit trees that will enable the 
students to have access to fresh organic 
fruit all year round. 
Once again the students will be 
involved in all facets of the program. 
Students and staff are looking forward to 
the first sod being turned. 
Finally, the school has also had 
initial discussions with the local Landcare 
group, who are involved in a wide range 
of projects in the local area. Plans are 
being put together that would see a pilot 
program initiated, enabling students to 
play an active role in growing, planting 
and regeneration programs in and around 
the school under the guidance of Landcare.
Graham Roberts

It’s been a bit quiet in Bermagui the 
last few weeks. Maybe it is because of that 
extremely hot weather we were having 
and everyone just staying inside to sit it 
out. However, I think we got off rather 
lightly compared to other areas in NSW. 
Our temperatures were mild compared 
to those over 45-degree temperatures 
inland. And even those in Sydney were 
staggering. I feel very lucky I made the 
move from western Sydney to this little 
piece of paradise on the coast and felt for 
friends and family back in the sweltering 
conditions. That drop of rain was sure 
welcome, topping up a lot of empty tanks. 
We can always count on the dog show to 
bring the rain.
Again we have had lots of movements 
in Lamont Street. The new shops in the 
old IGA building are powering ahead, and 
how good it looks now a new window is 
in. The Oyster Room closed its doors, but 
soon Corkies Café will be moving into the 
newer premises. The arcade is also looking 
a bit bare, with the Books by Sea store 
closing down. However, Michelle and Meg 
at Stitch Connection want to let everyone 
know they are still there happily working 
away at the end of the arcade.
We all need water!
While human residents made sure 
they are well hydrated during the hot 
spell, Wildlife Rescue South Coast were 
spreading the message that our native 
wildlife needed water as well.  Many 
people have birdbaths in their gardens to 
encourage our native birds, but consider 
other wildlife and maybe put some water 
bowls on the ground so that kangaroos, 
possums and even our favourite old 
“blueys” can take a drink from time to time.
Summer holidays are always a busy 
time for wildlife rescuers, with holiday 
makers from the cities totally unaware of 
the dangers faced when driving through 
our forests.  Collisions with kangaroos and 
other wildlife increase.  If you have guests 
from city areas, please ensure they are well 
aware of the dangers of hitting wildlife on 
our roads. They often drive way too fast 
during the vulnerable hours of dusk and 
far too many road kills are seen during the 
summer holidays.
As you read this – how about getting 
your mobile phone out right now and 
entering the Wildlife Rescue’s phone 
number – 0417 238 921.  You never know 
when you may come across injured or sick 
native animals and remember, this is their 
home as well as ours.

Bermagui’s Bellbird accommodation amongst the nation’s best!
Bermagui has been placed front and 
centre in recent worldwide accommodation 
awards.
The world’s largest online travel 
agency, booking.com, recently announced 
its 2016 Guest Review Awards for 
excellence in providing “great and truly 
memorable guest experiences”. From 
over 100 million reviews worldwide, the 
highest level of excellence attainable is a 
perfect score, 10/10 and in 2016 only 12 
accommodation providers in the whole 
of Australia, over all accommodation 
types (hotels, motels, B&Bs, holiday 
accommodation) scored a perfect 10, 
putting them in a select group of luxury 
places to stay.
One of these twelve is our very own 
Bellbird Cottage B&B in Bermagui, having 
scored a perfect 10 for two successive 
years. Bellbird is one of only two NSW 
accommodation providers to be on this 
exclusive list.
Speaking with husband and wife 
team, Gretel and Tony, who own and run 
Bellbird B&B, it is quickly apparent that 
they love what they do. Similarly, guests 
share that same passion and many of them 
come back again and again. 
Bellbird has a strong focus on the 
guest experience and provides a high 
quality, personal and very private stay. 
Nestled in just over an acre of spotted 
gum forest, within earshot of the sea, guests 
love the privacy and luxury of being just 
a couple of minutes from the harbour, yet 
feeling miles away from the bustle of their 
normal day.
Gretel and Tony love nothing more 
than spoiling their guests, bringing to 
their B&B their own international travel 
experiences combined with their love of the 
region, local produce and home cooking.
“We came to Bermagui 13 years 
ago, and the dream was always to have a 
B&B that would provide an extra special 
experience for visitors to our town and 
region,” they said. “This is the second year

Master Plan a great chance to beautify Bermagui
In 2018, Bermagui has a good chance 
of receiving over $500,000 in Bega Valley 
Shire Council funding to implement 
a CBD Master Plan to beautify and 
improve the pedestrian safety of the 
town centre. In 2013, Council employed 
SPIIRE, a town planning firm, to develop 
CBD Master Plans for Bega, Bermagui, 
Eden and Merimbula. After undertaking 
exhaustive community consultations, 
SPIIRE produced four plans, which Bega 
Valley Shire Council approved in early 
2016. Many in the community were closely 
involved in consultations with SPIIRE.
Philip Cox, a well-known architect 
who designed Bermagui’s Fishermen’s 
Wharf, the Sound Shell at Four Winds and 
the re-designed Bermagui Woolworths, 
which was done without payment, 
contributed the original concept for 
Lamont Street for the Bermagui Master 
Plan to SPIIRE, also pro bono. Its main 
design features include a colonnade 
mirroring the Fishermen’s Wharf with sails 
above, wider pavements for outdoor eating, 
planter boxes for shrubs and a consistent 
colour palette. Bunga Street will benefit 
from wider pavements and appropriate 
tree plantings. Several safer road crossing 
points are also included.
Derek van Bracht, the Council officer 
in charge of this process, recently confirmed 
that the Master Plans are high level 
designs which set out the major elements 
and directions on which more detailed 
landscape and construction designs will 
be based. He indicated scope certainly 
exists to improve detailed elements of 
the final plans. This can happen through 
further community consultation with the 
design teams. 
Bega Valley Shire Council has almost 
implemented Eden’s Master Plan, which 
the Eden Chamber of Commerce believes 
is very successful. Council has indicated 
Bermagui is likely to be the next town 
to receive funding because major road 
works underway in Bega and Merimbula 
this year must 
be completed 
before they can 
commence their 
Master Plans. 
At its last 
m e e t i n g o n 
1 F e b r u a r y, 
Council passed 
a motion, moved 
by Merimbula-
based Councillor 
Mitchell Nadin, 
that the draft 
2017/18 budget development include 
seeking expressions of interest for detailed 
designs, based on the current Merimbula 
and Bermagui CBD Masterplans, to 
establish working plans in consultation 
with the respective communities.
Some commercial building owners 
in Bermagui have expressed concern 
they will be forced to pay up to $10,000 
to connect underground power, as shown 
on the Master Plan. Derek van Bracht 
has indicated that at a minimum, Council 
would place conduit within any new paving 
or footpaths to allow for future powerline 
burial. However, in implementing its 
Master Plan, the Eden community opted 
not to bury its power lines, due to the 
expense. So this would be a future decision 
for the community and affected building 
owners to make at the detailed design stage.
If Council allocates funds for detailed 
scoping and costing studies for Bermagui’s 
Masterplan, the Bermagui Chamber of 
Commerce plans to hold workshops with 
interested business owners to seek their 
input into these studies. The Chamber of 
Commerce and National Trust Far South 
Coast Branch also would like to join other 
local groups to hold a community meeting 
to discuss the plans and ensure the detailed 
designs reflect our community’s needs. 
The Triangle’s website has a link to 
the Bermagui Master Plan: go to www.
thetriangle.org.au and click on ‘Off the 
page’.
Frances Perkins

“The Pilates Method of Body 
Conditioning develops the body uniformly, 
corrects posture, restores vitality, 
invigorates the mind and elevates the 
spirit.”
- Joseph H. Pilates
Pilates is a system of exercise that 
works to improve flexibility and build 
strength in the body. The movements 
emphasise alignment, coordination, 
breathing, balance and core muscular 
development, and range in difficulty from 
beginner-level exercises to more advanced 
movements. Pilates exercises can be 
modified to meet the specific needs of 
an individual and are often used for pain 
management, rehabilitation and physical 
therapy.
Practising Pilates also helps to 
develop concentration and focus the mind 
because each exercise is composed of 
highly precise movements. The focal point 
of Pilates is called the ‘Powerhouse’ and 
includes the muscles in the centre of the 
body, such as the abdominals, back and 
hips. Strong core muscles are critical for 
stability and allow for greater control of all 
bodily movements.
A regular Pilates practice strengthens 
both the mind and the body, leaving one 
with a sense of increased vigour and health.
Pilates classes will be held at the 
Cobargo School of Arts Hall starting on 5 
April, and running every Wednesday from 
6 to 7 pm. Classes are appropriate for any 
level student. For more information or 
to make a booking, please contact Eryn 
Culotta on 0432 369 655 or eryn.e.culotta@

Mindfulness is the process of bringing 
our attention to the internal and external 
experiences occurring in the present. 
We begin to discover the effects these 
have on our mind and body, and learn 
techniques that can transform these harmful 
impacts to more 
positive outcomes. 
This practice of 
Mindfulness is 
proven to many 
therapeutic effects, 
such as delaying 
or stopping the onset of any mind-
degenerative diseases such as Alzheimer’s, 
and helpful in the treatment of mental 
health conditions such as depression and 
anxiety. At the very least, Mindfulness 
can assist in making better choices in our 
daily life.

A look into the Seaside Fair: Christine Bimson 
When looking to put down permanent 
roots, Christine Bimson and husband Tony 
looked no further than Bermagui, moving 
into their home in June 1999.
Christine spent the first six years 
running a business from home before 
taking on a part time job with the Bermagui 
Visitor Information Centre. She then went 
on to work with the Bega District News, 
and later at Marshall and Tacheci Real 
Estate in Bermagui.
In 2011, Christine was again 
appointed Manager of the Bermagui 
Visitor Information Centre (VIC) and 
has remained there to the present time. 
She said, “It is very easy to love your job 
when you love where you live”. The busy 
Visitor Centre, auspiced by the Bermagui 
Chamber of Commerce and Tourism, has 
gone from strength to strength in the last 
few years and has received high reviews 
and acclaim on internet travel sites such as 
Trip Advisor. However, Christine has often 
commented that the VIC would not be such 
a success if it were not for the dedication, 
the commitment and the professionalism of 
the volunteers, who keep the centre open 
seven days a week.
Despite Christine’s busy work life, 
she somehow manages to find the time to 
be an integral part of the annual Bermagui 
Seaside Fair. Chris became involved with 
the Fair in 2003 after meeting with then 
Chairperson, Errol Masterson, who asked 
her if she would like to help out at an 
“event”. Little did she know that this event 
would become a lifelong passion. She 
recalls that her first ‘job’ with the Seaside 
Fair was shovelling ice into lots of bags and 
dragging them over to the oval.
The organising committee are known 
officially as the Bermagui Seasiders Inc, 
and are a non-profit group whose charter 
is to raise funds for local youth and 
community projects. When the concept of 
a fair was developed back in 1987, it was 
decided to host the event during the off-
peak tourist season as a way to encourage 
more visitors and to take advantage of 
the wonderful weather we can provide in 
early autumn.
Christine joined the committee 
in 2004 and later took on the role of 
Treasurer and Co-ordinator of stallholder 
applications, and any other job going! 
When long term Chairperson, Jo Jacobs, 
resigned in 2015, Christine then took on 
the role of Chairperson.
The Seaside Fair, now in its 20th year, 
has become firmly ingrained in Bermagui’s 
culture. Each year it follows a successful 
model, but is always open to something 
new. Three years ago, Denis Olmstead 
brought the idea of umbrellas at Bermagui 
to the committee. This project captured 
everyone’s imagination and we have been 
witness to some of the most creative and 
innovative pieces of art ever seen. The 
auctioning of the umbrellas during the 
Seaside Fair has alone raised over $14,000 
for local youth activities.
Although Christine’s involvement 
and workload for the Seaside Fair is huge, 
she wants to acknowledge that there are 
many others who share this workload every 
year. There are only six members that make 
up the organising committee, which is quite 
amazing when you think of the amount 
of work that needs to be done. However, 
Christine explained that there is a small 
army of volunteers who help on the day 
and highlighted the likes of Marine Rescue 
spending  the day just emptying the rubbish 
bins and the Cobargo scouts, who camp out 
on the oval overnight to provide security 
for the stalls and other equipment that is 
set up the day before and of course who 
would forget all those helpers who butter 
the bread, cook the sausages, look after the 
exhibitions and those that just generally run 
around and troubleshoot all day.
She also went on to say that, “the 
financial and in-kind support from local 
businesses is an integral part of the fair” 
and wanted to thank each and every 
business that is involved.
The 20th Seaside Fair on 11 March 
2017 is shaping up to be one of the best 
with an amazing line up of entertainment, 
and with a theme like “Mad Hatters Party”, 
the street parade should be a huge success.
Carolyne Banados

On The Yuin Trail
Local residents and visitors alike are 
beginning to notice the beautiful signs 
that are drawing attention in Bermagui, 
Fairhaven and Wallaga Lake. There are 
new “Welcome to Djiringaji Country” 
signs at the entrance to Bermagui. These 
signs feature the black duck, which is the 
totem of the Yuin people. 
There are nine other large, colourful 
signs placed in strategic locations 
throughout the town and at Wallaga 
Lake, which tell the stories, the legends, 
the history and recent achievements of 
the Djiringanj clan of the Yuin Nation, 
who have been custodians of this area for 
around 50,000 years.
 The Bermagui area has many sacred 
and significant sites for the Yuin nation. 
One of the largest midden sites in NSW, 
covering over seven acres and four metres 
deep, is located at the end of Fairhaven 
Point. Murunna Point was another 
important ceremonial site with clans 
travelling from other regions including 
the Monaro to attend gatherings and 
corrobborees there. All this information is 
explained on the signs.
The Yuin Cultural Heritage Trail was 
initiated by the Far South Coast Branch of 
the National Trust and Merrimans Land 
Council. The project was jointly funded by 
the National Trust Far South Coast Branch 
and the Mumbulla Foundation.  
Frances Perkins, Chairperson of the 
National Trust Far South Coast Branch, 
said she is very grateful for all the fantastic 
collaboration and help received for the 
project over the last four years. She would 
like to acknowledge the huge contributions 
from Merrimans Land Council elders, 
particularly Lynne Thomas, daughter of 
revered elder Guboo Ted Thomas, who 
advised on the signs locations and provided 
the legends and history told on the signs. 
The Bermagui Public School was also 
an enthusiastic partner and the children 
provided the beautiful artwork. 
Bega Valley Shire Council, particularly 
Graham Moore, Aboriginal Liaison Officer, 
provided valuable information and Council 
were very supportive, as was the NSW 
Office of Environment and Heritage. 
Frances also acknowledged the 
“heroic job” done by the Wallaga Lake/
Bermagui Men’s Shed, the members of 
which spent several weekends installing 
the signs in very hard and dry earth. 
Men’s Shed President Bill Johnston 
said, “Being part of this project has 
been a real pleasure for those involved, 
whilst giving the fellows a much better 
understanding of Yuin culture and 
heritage”.
There is a pamphlet outlining the 
location of the new signs on the Yuin 
Cultural Heritage Trail available at the 
Bermagui Visitors Centre.

Simple Sponge
Nothing nicer than a slice of fluffy 
sponge cake with cream and fresh summer 
fruits.
Replace flour with Gluten Free 
alternative. Quick and easy to ‘whip up’.
4 eggs separated (fresh from local chooks, 
so much nicer). Take out of the fridge 
earlier to reach room temperature.
¾ cup caster sugar
1/3 cup each plain, SR and cornflour. 
½ tsp baking powder (or use GF alternative)
½ tsp vanilla paste
Beat egg white till soft peaks and 
slowly fold in caster sugar. When well 
incorporated add egg yolks one at a time, 
then vanilla.
Remove beaters,  sift in the flours then 
fold gently together.
Lastly add a tablespoon of hot water 
to the mix and fold in. 
Prepare your preferred tin with 
cooking spray and baking paper 
and spoon in the mixture. Tap the 
tin slightly to evenly distribute the 
mixture.
Bake in a pre-heated oven, 
medium-hot, for approximately 20-25 
minutes. The cake should have risen 
and be a pale golden colour and come 
away slightly from the side of the tin. 
Do the skewer test to see if 
cooked though.
Leave to cool  in the tin then turn 
onto a rack.
Fill/top with cream and fresh 
summer fruit. Passionfruit is my 
favourite at the moment.
