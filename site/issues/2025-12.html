Another classic success
Waking up 
early Saturday, 1 
November, the rain 
had eased to fog in 
the sleepy town of 
Cobargo. A gentle 
rumble of cars, 
trucks, bikes and 
trailers carrying 
some awesome 
machinery could 
be heard as they 
made their way 
to the Cobargo 
Showgrounds.
At the stroke 
of 6.00 am the gates 
opened and the 
main arena began filling fast with a magnificent array of chrome, 
colour and craftsmanship. Some were wiping the early morning 
mist off their masterpiece with pride, gentle chatter became a 
constant sound of laughter and ‘G’day mate’ throughout the day.
By mid-morning it was time to wander through the swap 
meet stalls of car parts, bike leathers and gadgets that you just 
simply have to have, while others browsed the market stalls. You 
could smell the bacon and eggs cooking in the canteen, getting 
everyone’s taste buds ready for the food trucks setting up around 
the grounds.
The day then started for the three little ponies waiting patiently 
for the kids to work out they could actually get on and have a ride 
for free! Many going back again and again because it was so much 
fun. Then, to top off their day, a trip to see Netty and pick out 
something fabulous to 
have painted on their 
faces, from flowers to 
animals. They all ran 
around with smiles, 
before ending the day 
with a whopper ice 
cream.
Approximately 
450 entrants were 
judged in different 
categories, and about 
3,000 spectators 
strolled through the 
gates raising $22,000. 
This was distributed, 
after running costs 
were taken out, to the 
Cobargo Scouts ($7,000), Nardy House ($7,000), Tilba Public 
School P&C ($2,000) and Cobargo Pony Club ($1,000). In total 
$31,000 has been donated to local community organisations from 
the past two car shows!
The Far South Coast Classics Car Club would like to thank 
everyone who participated in this event by entering, buying a 
raffle ticket or just coming for a look. We also remind everyone 
to support our sponsors and volunteers, as without them days like 
this can’t happen! 
Mel Britton, President 
Far South Coast Classics Car Club

Editor,
The decision by five councillors to push for a large two-
lane concrete bridge despite Council’s community consultation 
showing overwhelming support for a single-lane concrete option 
is regrettable.
Whether Council is successful in securing government funds 
for the build is another matter.
Should such a high-speed, two-lane bridge ever be built, 
inexorably there will be a likely unstoppable push to replace the 
old single-lane timber bridges at the Murrah, Wapengo and Tanja 
with similar constructions.
The present low-speed road connecting Bermagui and Tathra 
passes through both Biamanga and Mimosa National Parks, Flora 
Reserves and is adjacent to magnificent coastal landscapes.
As such it has much to offer the local economy through 
tourism.
Encouraging faster through-traffic runs the risk of effectively 
duplicating the Princes Highway with the consequent problems 
of gridlocked traffic in Bermagui and, to a lesser extent, Tathra.
Jack Miller
Bermagui

Thanks for your local stories and 
photos! We love them and they make 
The Triangle our very own. Just a 
few tips for submitting stories and 
photos...
1. Stories should be 300 words 
or less except by prior arrangement.
2. Images and graphics should be 
sent as attachments – not embedded 
into your story. Please send the 
original digital photo or image, 
uncompressed, so we have a large 
file to work with. Generally above 
1 mb is large enough. Accepted 
formats include jpg, jpeg, png and 
pdf. Please include a caption for 
your photo at the bottom of the 
article it accompanies. Contact us 
for assistance.

other TEXT documents. 
4. Please do not send posters 
or flyers! Instead, write a few 
paragraphs about your event and 
include the date, time and venue 
in that. And attach a photo if you 
have one.
5. Think about a headline for 
your story. Please don’t leave it to us!

22nd of the month. Please email

Bowerbird Community Op Shop awards
The Bowerbird Community Op 
Shop’s tenth anniversary Community 
Association Awards (CAA) were presented 
at the Cobargo School of Arts Hall at an 
afternoon tea on Sunday, 26 October 2025.
The final votes were closely guarded 
secrets that added to the atmosphere as the 
individual awards were announced.
A gathering of almost fifty community 
members applauded as Quaama PS P&C, 
Friends of Cobargo Cemetery, Cobargo 
Pony Club and Narooma High School 
Cattle Team each received $500; Bermagui 
SLSC, 1st Scouts Cobargo and CAN Assist 
each received $1000; Cobargo School of 
Arts Hall received $2,500; and Volunteer 
Rescue Association Bega received $5,000.
Finally, Marine Rescue Bermagui 
received the top grant of $10,000. As 
awards MC Trisha Olsen said, ‘These 
community-initiated awards have proved 
a great success and, while they were 
considered a one-off event to celebrate 
our tenth anniversary, we may consider a 
similar format for future awards rounds.’
An additional feature of the afternoon 
was that some the awards were presented 
by Ros Hewitt to mark her retirement 
from the Bowerbird. Ros has now finally 
retired, sold up and will soon move to West 
Wyalong where her early roots were.
Current Bowerbird Committee 
Chair Corrine Harris said, ‘Ros was one 
of the founders of the Bowerbird and 
will be sorely missed by all. She has 
witnessed the growth of the organisation 
and provided both leadership and energy 
to its development. Well done, Ros!’
So, is that it for the Bowerbird year? 
Absolutely not!
Building on the success of previous 
years, the Bowerbird Op Shop is again 
hosting a Community Christmas lunch on 
25 December 2025! You will find more 
information in a separate article in this 
edition of The Triangle.
Steve Williams

Pickleball competition – Sapphire Coast vs Narooma 
On 7 November, thirty enthusiastic pickleball players 
gathered at Bermagui Sports Stadium for a friendly morning of 
sport, connection and community.
Sapphire Coast Pickleball Association Inc. (SCPAI) invited 
the newly formed Narooma Pickleball Association to Bermagui 
for a few fun-filled hours on the courts – and it turned out to be 
an absolute hit. There were medals, prizes, laughter and plenty of 
great rallies as friendly competition added to the fun.
Players shared their thoughts throughout the day:
‘It’s surprising how people change when a little competition is 
added into the mix!’
‘Everyone is so friendly and the match-ups are really good – 
I feel like I’ve played some great games.’
‘The stadium is amazing … the floor is so perfect!’
Between matches, there was plenty of time to mingle, enjoy 
the delicious food everyone brought to share and soak up the 
welcoming atmosphere, both on and off the court.
More feedback from participants included:
‘What a wonderful way to socialise with like-minded, passionate 
pickleballers. You made the new players feel so welcome. The 
pickleball spirit is well and truly high on the coast!’
‘Thanks for coordinating today – it was so much fun! Loved 
playing all different abilities. The Round Robin format was fast, 
fair and worked perfectly.’
‘The prizes were awesome – loved the medals and vouchers!’ 
A huge thank you to both clubs for making the day such a 
success and for showcasing the inclusive community spirit that 
makes pickleball on the coast so special.
New players are always welcome! Sapphire Coast Pickleball

are perfect for beginners. For all other skill levels, check the 
website for additional session times.
For details, go to the Sapphire Coast Pickleball website or 
follow us on Facebook. For more information on playing times 
at the Narooma Pickleball Association put ‘revolutioniseSPORT

Much to be found at The Foundry
A beautiful new space has sprung up 
in Bermagui, celebrating local talent and 
creativity.
After meeting at various local 
markets, local creators Anne, Alison, 
Cheryl, Corey and Jen realised they shared 
a desire to collaborate on a retail maker’s 
space – somewhere they could showcase 
their creations to customers all year round.
The next step was finding the right 
location and, after a year-long search, the 
ideal space in Bermagui finally appeared.
The Foundry@Bermagui opened on 
1 September on the corner of Lamont and 
Wallaga Street, immediately delighting 
locals and visitors alike. The studio and 
retail shop embraces colourful, vibrant 
clothing, homewares and gifts with a 
focus on thoughtful, enduring designs 
that emphasise quality, individuality and 
anti-disposability. You can also pick up 
a unique piece of fabric for your next 
sewing project, including limited edition 
Australian designer prints, French linen 
and Japanese denim.
Two months on, the collective has 
expanded from five to ten makers, now 
featuring leatherwork, natural skincare, 
handmade buttons, baskets, gift cards and 
more – an ever-growing range.
Responding to high demand, The 
Foundry now also offers a made-to-
measure clothing and soft furnishings 
service. This provides a cost-effective and 
convenient option for local residents, as 
well as visitors via the mail-order service. 
With a dedicated studio space and 
a platform for hosting workshops, The 
Foundry will announce a series of classes 
in the new year for participants keen to 
learn or refine their handmade skills.
Central to the ethos of the makers 
is the use of eco-friendly, sustainable 
materials, including repurposed quality 
fabrics wherever possible. The shop also 
donates 5% of its net profits to Women’s 
Community Shelters NSW.
Come and explore The Foundry@
Bermagui and discover the art of handmade 
fashion and gifting – because life is too 
short for dull colours and generic designs.
For more information you can email 
thefoundry2546@gmail.com. 
Jen

Mumbulla School awards second full financial scholarship
Mumbulla School for Rudolf Steiner Education in Bega 
is proud to announce the awarding of its second full financial 
scholarship, granted to a student who will commence kindergarten 
in 2026. 
The Mumbulla School Scholarship Program was established 
to broaden access to a Steiner education by offering full financial 
support to families who may not otherwise have the means to 
enrol. The recipient, who remains anonymous, will be supported 
throughout their entire schooling journey at Mumbulla School. 
This scholarship reflects the school’s commitment to inclusivity 
and the value of making Steiner education accessible to more 
families within the local community. 
‘We are delighted to be in a position to offer this opportunity,’ 
said Reagan White, Education Manager of Mumbulla School. 
‘Supporting a family in this way aligns deeply with our values and 
mission. We believe every child should have access to education 
that nurtures their individuality, creativity and connection to the 
world.’
This is the second time the school has awarded a full financial 
scholarship, marking a growing effort to ensure that financial 
circumstances are not a barrier to receiving a rich and holistic 
education. 
Mumbulla School extends a warm welcome to the scholarship 
recipient and their family and looks forward to supporting their 
learning and growth in the years ahead.

info@mumbullaschool.com.au or search for ‘Mumbulla School’ 
in your usual browser.

Medical emergencies/evacuations pack
To save stress during an emergency, 
prepare the things you may need to gather 
quickly for yourself or someone who is 
ill or infirm. It’s really helpful for ambos, 
nurses and other medical staff if you have 
a laminated card that lists your (or your 
person’s) communication or care needs. 
This is especially helpful for those with 
dementia, stroke etc.
On the card, write the contact details 
of your (or your person’s) regular GP, any 
allergies, conditions or recent surgeries, 
plus the contact details of next-of-kin 
and/or carers and how to access an 
Advance Care Directive if there is one. 
You may need to update the cards 
periodically, but it’s especially important 
to have an up-to-date card in case you 
become separated from your person or 
carer for any reason. 
In addition, you may need the 
following items.
Medicines. Take all medicines in 
their original packaging to ensure correct 
dosage (our brains can become foggy under 
stress). If you have a Webster-Pak or dose 
administration aid, take them, plus keep a 
small cooler bag ready to go for medicines 
that need it. Store all of these in one place. 
You can keep a pack with a second set of 
everything – but remember that medicines 
all have expiration dates that also need to 
be managed.
Medicines may include tablets and 
capsules, mixtures, inhalers and puffers, 
creams, ointments and patches, sprays, 
injections, any medicines bought over 
the counter without a prescription from a 
pharmacy or supermarket, plus vitamins, 
herbal or alternative medicines.
Aids. Have any mobility and other 
aids such as walking sticks, sitting aids, 
hearing aids and chargers, eye-glasses, 
portable breathing units, etc. handy.
Personal items. Make sure that you 
pack personal items such as dentures 
and cleaner, toiletries (toothpaste/brush, 
deodorant, soap, shampoo, hairbrush and 
shaving gear); a spare set of comfortable 
clothes, especially extra underwear; towels 
and face washers; lip balm, moisturiser, 
sanitary and incontinence items (can be 
difficult to acquire if shops are closed); 
car and house keys (label them), phones, 
laptops and chargers. ALWAYS have 
bottled water in the car or in your bag.
Have a safe and joy-filled holiday 
season everyone.
Shanna Provost

Creative dissenter Honor Northam
We can thank Honor’s mother for 
bringing her and Tim to Bermagui. After 
leaving Lord Howe Island, they wanted 
to open a restaurant somewhere small and 
beautiful by the beach. On the long slow 
drive from Brisbane, they looked at many 
towns but realised Bermi had everything, 
‘and why would I not choose the place 
where my mother lived?’ 
Long lines of locals and visitors at 
Honorbread speak to the delicious success 
of this local business. Honor and Tim 
hope their fifty employees will soon own 
it, using a model that is gaining traction 
in Europe and the US. Honor explains, 
‘This model seems complicated at first, but 
selling your business to your employees 
through an Employee Ownership Trust 
makes a lot of sense. It keeps the business 
in the hands of people who care about it 
and know how to run it. A constitution 
grounds important values, the Trust owns 
the business on behalf of employees, and 
future profits pay off the owners and later 
get dispersed to employees. A company 
board makes sure the company still works, 
it’s not a co-op, but employees have more 
of a say. It would make an enormous 
difference if the Treasurer and the ATO 
would issue guidelines about what it 
would mean tax wise, about what kind of 
valuation I need. Then my lawyer and my 
accountant could just set it up.
‘We’ve worked really hard to build 
certain values into this business. It’s not 
easy when you have so many different 
personalities and needs. Every day you 
have to think and act on the right thing, 
right here and right now. That doesn’t just 
happen. You have to constantly reinvent 
what’s going to work. When we started a 
decade ago, Tim and I were making every 
decision, but now we are slowly stepping 
back and a management team is doing 
much more of that work.’
Honor has recently joined the Four 
Winds Board, ‘I love everything about 
Four Winds, which has evolved so much 
since we first got here fifteen years ago. The 
classical music festival every two years 
was fantastic and introduced me to lots of 
music I would never have heard. Now that 
opportunity happens all year round. That 
space is being used and it’s alive. People’s 
ideas are being incorporated. I like things 
that bring people together, share creativity 
and allow people to grow. I’m seeing all 
that happen at Four Winds and I love being 
part of it.’
Honor recently had the opportunity to 
visit Scott Reef, endangered by resource 
giant Woodside’s proposal for gas 
extraction in the remarkable Kimberley 
region. ‘It’s really hard to get there. Scott 
Reef is 400 nautical miles west of Broome. 
The exact location of where they want to 
mine is the most pristine reef. There was 
bleaching everywhere else, but not there, 
and no one would see that. We were with 
a team documenting the beauty and the 
danger. Let’s protect this precious place 
from seismic blasting and from industrial 
gas extraction. We need to be louder. We 
saw another former mine, and it was just 
abandoned. Nobody sees the destruction or 
the waste and that’s what they’re banking 
on. I’ve never explored Western Australia 
before, so it was incredible. We saw 
crocodiles and incredible First Nations 
rock art, just extraordinary.’
Honor has a new project in a café 
space a few doors down from Honorbread 
called The Dissenters Lounge. ‘I called 
it The Dissenters Lounge because of a 
place I saw in the UK and also because I 
sometimes worry that we’re moving into 
a time where people aren’t speaking up 
for what’s right. They’re scared. I’ve been 
thinking a lot about homelessness too, so I 
wanted to make a space where people can 
have comfort, clean clothes, internet and 
a space to sit at a nice table, have a cup of

Barranguba class steps back in time at Montreal Goldfield
On a November Wednesday, the 
students of Barranguba class embarked 
on an exciting journey to the Montreal 
Goldfield, immersing themselves in 
the history of the Australian gold rush. 
This hands-on excursion brought their 
classroom learning to life, transporting 
them back to the 1800s.
Led by knowledgeable guides, the 
class explored the historic goldfield site, 
gaining close-up views of the old mine 
shafts that once buzzed with activity. 
The students enthusiastically tried their 
hand at panning for gold, experiencing 
firsthand the challenges and thrills faced 
by prospectors of the era. In addition to 
mining, the day was filled with play using 
children’s toys from the time, offering a 
glimpse into life beyond the work.
One of the highlights was experiencing 
what schooling was like in the nineteenth 
century. After sampling lessons in a replica 
classroom, the students were unanimous in 
their relief to be attending school in 2025, 
appreciating the comforts and learning 
methods available today.
Throughout the excursion, the class 
learned about the wider impact of the 
gold rush on Australia’s growth, including 
how it fuelled immigration and shaped the 
nation’s development. They also reflected 
on the tough living and working conditions 
faced by the miners and their families.
The visit sparked curiosity about local 
history too, with students pondering the 
intriguing Mystery of Mystery Bay, adding 
an element of local lore to their educational 
adventure.
The Barranguba class returned to 
school inspired and full of stories, their 
understanding of Australian history 
enriched by this engaging and memorable 
experience.

rush, students have been writing letters 
and journal entries from the perspective

efforts to protect our community.
The communities supported by Tilba 
RFS (Central Tilba, Tilba Tilba, Akolele, 
Wallaga Lake and Mystery Bay) are 
spread across large tracts of agricultural 
and forested land and access to water in 
the vicinity of bushfires is challenging. 
In addition, mains supply is often not 
adequate during major bushfires. 
To address this problem, Tilba 
RFS has gained grant funding from the 
Community Building Partnership Fund, 
to purchase two high-volume water pumps 
that will allow faster, easier access to water 
from previously inaccessible sources. 
This in turn will improve the Brigade’s 
firefighting capabilities.
Community involvement is the key 
factor to the success of this initiative. To 
help build our capability, landholders with 
water resources in the Tilba RFS area can 
register their interest in a site visit. The 
purpose of these visits is to map and record 
water sources on rural properties and to 
gain agreement to access the water sources 
during bushfires.
The site visits will also provide 
opportunities for us to identify potential 
fire hazards onsite and provide advice on 
fire risk management to improve safety 
for you and firefighters in the event of a 
fire. Site visits would normally take 30-45 
minutes. Information collected at the time 
of the visit will be treated as confidential 
and only used to help firefighting efforts.

Cuttagee Bridge: a dark day for democracy
On 12 November, Bega Valley Shire Councillors Allen, 
Fitzpatrick, Nadin, Noble and Porter voted to replace Cuttagee 
Bridge with a two-lane concrete structure. Since Council said 
it intended to replace the bridge in 2021, there has been strong 
community support for a single-lane structure that reflects its 
heritage and aesthetic values and considers its fragile coastal 
environment.
Council dismissed a petition of 14,900 signatures because 
not all signatories were local. It then dismissed the results of 
its two most recent surveys showing around 70% supported a 
one-lane heritage outcome and 52% supported the one-lane 
design option because they reflected ‘locals with strong personal 
views and motivations’, not the sentiment of the broader shire.
Only 28% of respondents supported the two-lane concrete 
option. More than 60% said it didn’t reflect the local area’s 
character. The existing bridge calms traffic. The new one will 
likely have an 80 km speed limit with no load limit. It is a busy 
pedestrian area during school holidays without footpaths or safe 
access to the beach. The single-lane option would still have 
allowed emergency vehicles to pass other traffic.
In 2023, NSW Labor made a $15 million election 
commitment to restore the bridge to uphold its heritage value and 
community wishes. NSW Minister for Regional Transport and 
Roads Jenny Aitchison welcomed news of Council’s decision 
after an ‘extraordinarily long time’ but was disappointed the 
bridge’s heritage values were not reflected. Member for Bega 
Dr Michael Holland said Council had already spent $2 million.
The cost of the concrete bridge is estimated to be $29.1 
million, $2.4 million more than the single-lane option and 
more than double the $13.3 million spent restoring Wallaga 
Lake Bridge.
Council expects it will take 12 months to build the bridge, 
with completion due in mid-2027. Construction cannot begin 
until the $14 million shortfall is funded. Will a nine-metre wide 
bridge in this constrained area receive environmental approval? 
For context, Bermagui River Bridge is only eight metres wide.
Phil Coates

Cuttagee Bridge is over scaled for a secondary road
As most readers would now be aware, the Council decided at 
their meeting on 12 November 2025 to adopt Option 3 for Cuttagee 
Bridge – a concrete bridge with a nine-metre-wide bridge roadway 
and two and a half metre-wide shared pathway. The twelve-metre 
width of this proposed bridge appears to be very large for a bridge 
along a secondary regional road in a fragile environmental area.
As a point of comparison, refer to the photo of the Bermagui 
River bridge in Bermagui township with an eight-metre-wide 
bridge roadway and a 1.9-metre raised walkway – a total of 
just under ten metres wide. This bridge is completely adequate 
for much more two-way traffic flow than that envisaged for the 
12.2 metre-wide Cuttagee Bridge Option 3.
I am concerned that there is the potential for significant cost 
overruns in the construction of such a large bridge at Cuttagee, 
currently estimated to cost $29.1m. Council will need to seek 
additional funding above the $15m provided by the NSW 
Government. To reach this stage, I understand that Council 
has already spent around $2m on preliminary bridge design 
options and a community consultation process (the outcome of 
which Council largely ignored – only 28% of the responders 
wanted Option 3).
Council next faces a Review of Environmental Factors. 
Council is proposing inserting a concrete bridge (12.2 metres 
wide) and a single lane steel temporary bridge into a fragile lake 
estuary area close to the ocean (the current bridge is only five 
metres wide).
The steel temporary bridge is currently estimated to be 
$2.2m with only 25 metres of bridge and 160 metres of roadway 
approach, presumably across the beach. With only a 25-metre 
bridge component this must assume there will be no flooding, 
storms or ocean surge during the new bridge construction period 
of at least a year. The temporary bridge is mostly necessary to 
enable local residents and businesses access to Bermagui.
The community should be aware that with the proposed scale 
and likely further delays, there is the potential for the Cuttagee 
Bridge Renewal Project to result in a major cost overrun for our 
Council and ultimately the ratepayers.
Geoff Steel, Bermagui

What’s Growin’ On
Mark Evans
The colours of Christmas
Red, green and white are among 
the most popular colours associated with 
Christmas. This iconic combination is 
often linked to the English holly (Ilex 
aquifolium), with its evergreen leaves and 
bright red berries. Amid the European 
snow, holly symbolised hope during the 
depths of winter and was central to ancient 
solstice celebrations long before Christmas 
as we know it was celebrated.
In Australia, however, holly berries 
ripen between May and August, making 
the plant less suited to our December 
celebrations – unless you’re hosting 
Christmas in July. Despite the abundance 
of plastic replicas, holly isn’t a practical 
choice for an Aussie Christmas.
A plant that has been successfully 
adapted for our summer season is the 
poinsettia (Euphorbia pulcherrima). 
Native to Mexico, poinsettias naturally 
change colour in response to shorter 
winter days. Australian growers use light 
and temperature control to coax them into 
colour for December. If you replant your 
poinsettia in the garden, expect it to return 
to its natural winter-flowering cycle.
For a festive splash of red from 
closer to home, the Metrosideros species 
– commonly known as the New Zealand 
Christmas Bush – offers a vibrant, 
seasonally timed alternative. With 
evergreen foliage and fluffy red flowers 
that appear in early summer, these plants 
range from large trees such as M. excelsa 
to compact forms including ‘Little Bridget’ 
(1 m x 1 m), ‘Mini Christmas’ (2 m x 1 
m) and ‘Firecracker’ (4 m x 2 m). These 
smaller cultivars make excellent hedges or 
container plants.
Our most local and beloved 
Christmas plant is the NSW Christmas 
Bush (Ceratopetalum gummiferum) that 
grows naturally from Ulladulla to Evans 
Head. Like the poinsettia, its showy red 
colouring comes not from the flowers but 
from the enlarged calyx lobes that flush 
after flowering. This species has been a 
festive favourite since colonial times and 
was used in much the same way as holly in 
European traditions. Its bright red bunches 
make striking, long-lasting cut flowers. 
Cultivars such as ‘Albury’s Red’ and 
‘Johana’s Christmas’ are prized for their 
especially deep scarlet tones.
For an easy, cheerful burst of seasonal 
colour, try red and white petunias in 
baskets or pots. Pairing white petunias 
with red salvias also creates a bold, eye-
catching combination. Or keep an eye out 
for red-and-white bi-coloured petunias for 
an instant holiday feel.
Around town gardens, oleanders 
and bougainvillea are in full flush. One 
of our favourite seasonal colour pairings 
is coming to an end as the jacarandas and 
Illawarra flame trees finish their dramatic 
flower drop.
In the wild places – particularly 
along the Triangle roadsides – look for 
Ozothamnus diosmifolium, a two-metre 
native shrub carrying tight clumps of 
small, white, papery flowers at the ends 
of its branches.
In the vegie garden it’s still a great time 
to plant fast-growing summer favourites 
such as cucumbers, beans and zucchini, as 
well as blocks of corn, more tomatoes and 
pumpkins. With hot, dry conditions settling 
in, be sure to keep water up to the garden 
and mulch any bare soil. If plants begin to 
wilt, consider providing shade cloth during 
the hottest part of the day.
Any comments, gardening questions 
or plant or pest identification problems, 
send them through to gardening@
thetriangle.org.au. I will get back to you.
Happy growing and merry Christmas.

Roasted carrots  
with tahini sauce
1 kilo of carrots (not too big)
2 tablespoons olive oil
Sea salt
½ teaspoon cumin
1 teaspoon honey
1 orange
2 tablespoons chopped pistachios (shelled, 
roasted and salted)
1 tablespoon chopped chives
1/3 cup of plain Greek yoghurt
3 tablespoons of tahini
1 garlic clove, finely chopped or grated
Method:
Heat oven to 180-200º C.
Peel carrots and slice into spears. Toss the 
carrots with olive oil, salt and cumin. Roast 
in hot oven for about 20 to 25 minutes until 
they are soft, with a little bit of charring 
around the edges. Drizzle with a teaspoon 
of honey while hot and toss again.
Tahini sauce
Cut orange in half, squeeze 3 tablespoons 
of juice. Add yoghurt, tahini, garlic and 2 
tablespoons of water and whisk together 
in a bowl. Season to taste with salt and 
pepper. Smear the mixture onto a serving 
platter.
Give the roasted carrots a squeeze of 
orange juice, drizzle with a small amount 
of olive oil and a grind of black pepper. 
Toss and add extra seasoning if warranted.
Arrange the carrots on top of the smear 
of tahini sauce, pour any leftover oil 
and seasoning on top and sprinkle with 
chopped pistachios and chives. Serve hot, 
warm or at room temperature.

Green beans with garlic chips
These delicious green beans are for garlic 
lovers. When you put this dish together, 
you need to pay attention – be sure not to 
burn the garlic chips or they will become 
bitter and you’ll need to start again. And 
when you toss the beans, the oil should be 
hot but not smoking. This is a wonderful 
side dish for Christmas or any time green 
beans are in season.
500 grams green beans
6-8 large garlic cloves
2 tablespoons olive oil
2 tablespoons butter
Squeeze of lemon juice
Handful of finely chopped parsley
Salt flakes and freshly ground black pepper
Method: 
Peel the garlic cloves and slice them very 
thinly. Heat the oil and butter in a frying 
pan and fry the garlic chips on a medium 
flame until just turning golden. Remove 
them to a paper-lined plate. 
Top and tail the beans and steam or boil in 
salted water until al dente (5-7 minutes).
Drain and refresh the green beans in cold 
water. When you're ready to serve, reheat 
the garlic oil to medium high and sear the 
beans tossing them until they are a bit 
blistered. Squeeze lemon juice and give 
a final toss.
Plate the beans, pouring the garlic oil on 
top. Scatter the garlic chips and parsley, 
then grind some salt and pepper.
Serve hot.

Potato salad with fennel and apple
Cook about a kilogram of potatoes with 
skin on till tender. Cool and peel skin. Cut 
into smallish cubes. Dice some fennel into 
similar sized pieces (probably weighing 
half the amount of potato). Cube a similar 
amount of green apple (as the fennel), 
toss apple in lemon juice to stay white. 
Toss everything in a mix of ½ cup of 
mayonnaise (homemade or in a jar), ¼ 
cup of sour cream, add a spoonful of Dijon 
mustard, a spoonful of honey, chopped 
fresh dill and season with salt and pepper. 
Serves 4-6.
