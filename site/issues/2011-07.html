The ABC South East recently ran a
program highlighting concerns of
residents in Tathra regarding poor mobile
phone coverage through Telstra.  Mr Chris
Taylor, General Manager of Telstra
Countrywide was invited to discuss the
issue.  Mr Taylor maintained that only a
small pocket of Telstra customers were
affected.  Well, apparently the phones ran
hot with angry Telstra customers from all
over the area ringing in to complain about
poor mobile service.
I sent an email to the ABC telling
them about my Wireless Broadband
service in Fairhaven – which is practically
non-existent and the difficulties I have had
with Telstra trying to have this rectified.
My email was read out on air and the
answer given by Mr Taylor virtually said
if I was not happy, I could go to another
company!
I maintain that Telstra sold me a
service then failed to meet their obligation
to provide the service I was paying for. I
had to accept a Wireless service because
Telstra could not provide ADSL in my area.
The problem of poor signal was rectified
by the simple addition of an antenna,
which now gives me a good signal – but at
a cost to me!  My argument is that Telstra
should fulfil its obligation to provide the
service I am paying for – even if it means
providing an antenna as part of the
service.  My problem is now solved after
Mr Taylor rang me and has agreed to
refund the cost of the antenna “as a
gesture of good faith”.

be heard anyway.

email address) of Chris Taylor from
Telstra, who has said to me that he would
welcome any complaints from Telstra
customers, I am giving all our readers
in the Triangle the opportunity to
complain loud and long.

The Nature Coast Marine Group, a
community organisation based in the
Eurobodalla, has been awarded a funding
Marine research under way
grant to carry out surveys of marine life
on reefs in and around the Batemans
Marine Park. Funding from the
Australian Government’s Caring for our
Country Program. The grant will enable
divers from the NCMG to survey at least
16 sites from Mollymook to Bermagui
twice over the next 12 months.
NCMG President Jenny Edwards
described the grant as an important
development for the group, as it builds
on an existing survey program carried
out by NCMG snorkelers and will enable
the group to develop capacity to carry
out more complex research into the
future. “We are pleased that the
Southern Rivers CMA has shown
confidence in our group’s work. We also
welcome the scientific supervision of the
project by the Batemans Marine Park and
the Reef Life Survey program of the
Tasmanian Aquaculture and Fisheries
Institute.”
Project managers Andrew Green
and Bill Barker said the project is already
well under way. Two training programs
An inquisitive cuttlefish interrupts Bill Barker’s survey
work (Photo: Andrew Green) 
have been held to train eight divers in the
survey methodology and the first round
of surveys is approaching completion. The
project includes an education and
information component aimed at raising
community awareness of the marine
environment.
Project manager Bill Barker said
that gathering data on the marine
environment has always presented
challenges arising from weather and
underwater conditions, lack of trained
personnel and cost. “It is only recently,
with the recognition of threats to the
marine environment and the declaration
of many marine parks around Australia’s
coastline, that a larger scale effort has
begun aimed at building up a better
picture of the local marine environment
and its inhabitants. This grant will make a
contribution to other work to ensure that
data on the marine life of our coastline
will be part of this wider effort.”
Anyone interested in knowing
more should contact Bill Barker or Andrew
Green on info@ncmg.org.au.

signposted.

limits to all dogs due to its proximity to
the Biamaga National Park.  This is
incorrect.  Baragoot Beach is not located
in the Biamaga National Park but
adjacent to it.  This can be confirmed
with National Parks and Wildlife, and/or
a topographical map.
Also please note Baragoot Beach
is not designated by BVSC as a dog
prohibited area.
If 
necessary 
I 
would 
be
agreeable to discuss the foregoing with
the author of the article to clarify the
points made.

My Triangle
Phillip Norris, 47 years old, dairy farmer, Sam’s Creek.
How long have you lived in the
triangle?
40 years, the first 8 years were
in Bega, my grandparents had
a dairy farm there which my
parents used to work. It was
where the new Bega bridge is
now. They ran about 70 to 90
cows. The milk was picked up
in milk cans before we got a
vat in the 1970’s. I went to Bega
primary for my first two years
of school.
Where did you live after that?
After Bega we went to Rankins
Rd, Coolagalite, by then there
were 6 of us kids left. Seven years later the family moved to Barlows Rd, been
there ever since. Then I moved just around the corner, to Sam’s Creek with my
partner. Now I have two kids, Alexander and Eliza Jane. They’re pretty good, easy
going and don’t argue yet.
What do you do?
I’m back to dairying now, I ran beef cattle for 10 years but now I’m back into
dairying. The beef market was too volatile. Dairy cattle are a lot quieter. Also I
like to rear my own calves and build a herd. But its all hands on with dairy cattle -
much harder job.
What do you like the most about living in the triangle?
Its fairly quiet and peaceful. The locals are good.
What do you like the least about living in the triangle?
Newcomers. Half of them are from the city and they think they own the place.
What would you do/have to improve the place?
A good car park and decent public toilets in Cobargo. The council had the chance
to do something about it and they didn’t. Shame. Besides that, I like it the way it is,
except I’d like to see the shops open more often, especially on public holidays –
those are the best trading days, especially for the eating places and craft shops.
What changes have you seen over the time you’ve been here?
Lots of changes in the owners of the shops. More traffic heading out to Yowrie
and Wandella way. Lot more cars you pass and you don’t know who they are. Lot
more hobby farms around, that’s one thing that’s changed a lot, the smaller
holdings. It’s good because it brings in more money to the area, bad because
they don’t seem to care for the land so much.

Shortly Forests NSW will again be
logging in compartments 2001 and 2002,
an area covering both sides of the
Wallaga Lake Road at the Cobargo turn -

Logging in the area is a divisive
issue and because I oppose it I think it is
incumbent on me to justify my position.
There will never be agreement reached
by negotiation because there is no
negotiation and only a pretence at public
consultation. It is a battle for the life of the
forest in terms of biodiversity, ecosystem
services and carbon sequestration as
against short term gain for an industry that
mines the timber, burns the residues and
sends the product and the profits
overseas.  Let us be clear. A forest is a
long term, surviving, self regulating
ecological community. It is not a naturally
Jo Lewis
occurring plantation that can be
harvested at will. Logging as carried out
by Forests NSW is the destruction of that
forest community and death by a
thousand cuts.
We live in an area that should be
a massive biodiversity resource bursting
with wild life. Instead we have a remnant
Koala population hanging on for dear life,
small pockets of a surviving potoroo
population, maybe a few sugar gliders
and occasional wallabies. The spotted
quoll has probably gone and maybe the
bandicoots too. There are some
kangaroos and rabbits in increasing
numbers. Many bird species are
threatened 
or 
endangered 
and
indigenous plant species are overrun by
weeds.
We face a real possibility of
catastrophic climate change but we log
and burn a resource that could help us to
avoid it. It may not be widely known but
Professor Mackey from ANU has
demonstrated the huge potential of these
forests to act as a carbon sink that would
mitigate climate change by storing
carbon while we struggle to bring our
emissions under control.
We log and we burn. We can be
smarter than that.

Book Review
Dr. Heather O’Connor
Hazel Rowley, Franklin and Eleanor:
An Extraordinary Marriage,
Melbourne University Press, 2011,
$36.99
You might wonder what on earth
more could be said about the famous
Roosevelts, both having had thousands
of words written about them already. But
this is a biography with a difference; it’s
really a biography of a marriage, rather
than individual stories. I can vaguely
remember reading that there’s was an
unconventional arrangement, with FDR
having other long-term relationships
with women – indeed, Lucy Mercer was
with him when he died. Eleanor had
lesbian relationships, and it was not
unusual for her and Franklin to spend
long periods apart, particularly when she
started to emerge as a strong,
independent woman in her own right.
Throughout their long marriage, they
remained supportive and respectful of
each others work and life choices – truly
remarkable for the times, and given the
class from which they emerged.
His unprecedented four terms as
President covered the Great Depression
and the Second World War, an
extraordinary public life made more so
in the light of the onset of adult polio,
which is described with great accuracy
but 
which 
never 
descends 
to
sentimentality. The stress of his work,
his triumphs in pulling America through
the Depression and the War placed
enormous strains on the marriage, but
her support remained unwavering. In
part, it accounts for her ability to
continue a remarkable public life after
he died, although this is not covered in
any detail in the book – for that you have
to read her own biography.
It is a tragedy that Rowley died
recently; the world has lost a great
biographer. Anyone who could find a
new angle from which to tell the story of
the remarkable Roosevelts deserves all
the praise and accolades with which this
book was greeted. Don’t miss it.

Soft Footprint Recipes
Carole Broadhead
I enjoy this simple chicken dish that I
first tasted in Athens at a friends’ place
it is quick and delicious, one that the
children could make for dinner.
Heike’s chicken and potato bake
Judge the quantities that your family
will eat.
Ingredients;
Potatoes, peeled and diced about 2cm
square
Chicken legs or pieces
 1 tablespoon of olive oil,
 oregano or thyme and salt and pepper.
Turn the oven on to 180 degrees.
Place the potatoes in a bowl and drizzle
with oil and turn them with your hand to
make sure they are all coated. Add half
a teaspoon of herbs per person then
season well with salt and pepper
turning all the while. Then put
a little oil in your hands and
coat the chicken, season with
pepper and salt.
Place the potatoes in a large
baking dish making them into
a single layer. Put the chicken
on top so that the juices run
into the potatoes while
cooking.
Turn the chicken after twenty
minutes and put an egg slice
under the potatoes to loosen
them to make sure they don’t
stick.
Leave for a further twenty minutes,
check the chicken is cooked through
and the potatoes are cooked and
sticky.
Serve with a salad in the summer or
green vegetables in winter.
I like beans that have been sliced in
half lengthways so they are thin, put
into boiling water for three minutes or
so then drizzled with oil and served
with a wedge of lemon to squeeze over
the lot!
This dish is a real comfort food for us
and I hope you too will enjoy it,

On Saturday 21 May, a meeting
was held at the Bega Cheese Training
Centre to form a committee for the newly
established Far South Coast branch of the
Native Animal Network Association
[NANA]. There were 24 adults, 3 children,
1 young wombat and 1 young kangaroo
present at the meeting.
NANA has been active in our
region for many years, but up until now
the administration and meetings have all
been based in Nowra. Recently a decision
was taken to change the group’s name to
Wildlife Rescue South Coast. This
provided the impetus for the formation
of a local sub-branch, as many of our local
members wished to continue to operate
as NANA [pronounced Nan – ah].
A special meeting had previously
been held at Potaroo Palace in April.
Committee members from Nowra met
with local members to discuss the
formation of a Far South Coast sub-
branch and the feasibility of the sub-
branch continuing to use the name
NANA, whilst members further north
would operate under the new name. This
has now occurred.
Ray Alcock from Bemboka has
been our group’s Far South Coast Area
Co-ordinator for quite some time. He is
well known throughout the Bega Valley.
At the recent meeting in Bega he was
elected President and Head Species Co-
ordinator with unanimous acclaim and
approval expressed by all present. Other
positions on the committee were filled
by members from all around our local
membership area, which covers Mogo
to Eden.
Monthly meetings will be held at
1pm on the 3rd Saturday of every month.
These will be followed by training
sessions and social afternoon teas.
Interested members of the public are
invited to attend. Please contact our
Publicity Officer, Leonie Barrett on 6493
2574 for further information.

news of local rescues, and reports on
animals currently in care.
